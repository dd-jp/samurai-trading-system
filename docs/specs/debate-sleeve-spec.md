## 1. What the sleeve is

One debate per screened name per trading day, pre-open, on daily bars to the prior close plus news; a swing hold (days to weeks) closed by a broker-resting ATR stop or a time stop. The cycle's `tradingDate` is the entry session: every read uses bars strictly before it, a name whose last bar is more than five calendar days old is dropped, and so is one with fewer bars than a window it reads needs, with a bar on under 95% of that window's sessions, or with no bar on the last session, counted against SPY's calendar (the coverage invariant, postmortem §2; the 200-session SMA window is checked before any LLM call and skips as `window_coverage`; a SPY series with no bar in the last five days fails every window closed and is journalled as a `data` refusal; the risk module's ADV window keeps its own calendar-span rule, T8); the macro gate (§6) is keyed on the entry session. Long and short are each a counted trial against arm 2 (Q17 verdict, 2026-09-22). Judged by G1: ≥ 100 closed paper trades and a one-sided 95% test vs arm 2 before its 30% leaves cash.

Composition root: `server/apps/v2/index.ts`. The sleeve is `server/apps/v2/signal/debate-sleeve.ts` and registers with one line in `composeV2Root` (`registry.register(debateSleeve)`). The momentum sleeve is **not registered** (§8). The cycle (`server/apps/v2/cycle.ts`) runs once per trading date: sweep fills → fill simulated entries and flattens from earlier days (§7) → decide → per book cancel stale entries, exits, entries → sweep fills → mark every book.

## 2. Universe (G4 + G18)

Pool: current S&P 500 constituents (`data/bars/sp500-constituents.csv`, last row on or before the decision date), all on the Alpaca leg (`venueFor` maps every name to Alpaca). The LSE ETF/ETC leg is empty until the Saxo paper adapter is built over the 22 committed LSE lines (doc 70 §10.4; ruled 2026-09-25, doc 66, ADR §5 item 13); no Saxo adapter is built in this step (§7). Which venue carries which side of which asset is the route table below.

Per day, at most 20 names:

| Half | Rule | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Liquidity core (~10) | Top 10 by 20-day average dollar volume over the pool, from the same daily bars the debate reads; ties break alphabetically. | Ruled (G4). Built (`liquidityCore`). |
| Movers (~10) | **Pre-declared (trial T1, §5):** from the pool minus the liquidity core, the 10 names with the largest absolute prior-day close-to-close return, among names whose prior-day dollar volume (raw close × volume) is ≥ $50M (`MOVERS_MIN_DOLLAR_VOLUME_USD`); ties break alphabetically. Rationale: "movers" is the one reading of G4's wording that needs no input beyond the bars the debate already reads, so it is falsifiable today; the $50M floor is this sleeve's own choice, not borrowed from doc 70 (which sets no ADV floor): the largest order §5 produces is ~£100 of notional, so the floor is there only to keep anomalous thin prints (a halted or delisting name whose one-day return is an artefact) out of the top-10 ranking, not to protect fills. G18 1a's sentiment-assisted pick is a later trial once a sentiment writer exists (§3). | Built (`selectMovers`), counted as trial T1. |

### Venue routes (doc 66 CFD rulings 2026-09-28, [#1849](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1849))

Debate sleeve only; arm 2 shares the routes (it shares the universe and `actionFor`); the S2 rules candidates stay long-only in cash and ETFs. A route is a venue value: `alpaca`, `saxo` (cash, GBP), `saxo_cfd_gbp` and `saxo_cfd_usd` (CFDs, priced in the currency the name trades in). An instrument's data venue (which bar source and calendar it reads) is separate from its route.

| Asset | Long | Short |
|---|---|---|
| US single stock | Alpaca (`alpaca`) | Saxo CFD (`saxo_cfd_usd`) |
| UK single stock | Saxo CFD (`saxo_cfd_gbp`), no stamp duty | Saxo CFD (`saxo_cfd_gbp`) |
| UK ETF / ETC | Saxo cash (`saxo`) | Saxo CFD on the ETF where the catalogue lists one (`saxo_cfd_gbp`) |
| Index | 1x ETF at Saxo cash (`saxo`) | Saxo CFD on the index or the ETF |
| US ETF | Alpaca only if UK access is confirmed (unverified, Q2) | Saxo CFD (`saxo_cfd_usd`) |

Every CFD other than a UK single-stock long is a short. A held position keeps the venue it was opened on: exits, rearms and flattens read `Position.venue`, never the route table, because one instrument string can be long at Alpaca and short at Saxo. The route table is `server/apps/v2/data/venue-routes.ts`. Built rows: US single stock (home venue `alpaca`) and UK ETF / ETC (any other home venue). The UK single-stock, index and US-ETF rows are not built: the router has no kind for them until [#1866](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1866) and [#1914](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1914) add the names. Until #1866 builds the UK single-stock long row, the risk gate refuses every long on a CFD venue as `long_on_cfd`; that row must lift the refusal for its own kind only.

Bounds on the CFD routes, each ruled 2026-09-28 unless noted:

- **1x gross cap (T13).** A book's gross notional, cash and CFD, long plus short, never exceeds its equity. Short proceeds do not help: `applyFill` credits a short's proceeds to `cash_gbp`, so the cash gate alone would let a book short many times its equity. The risk gate owns the rule (`RiskGate.entryRoom` and `entryRoomRefusal`, `server/apps/v2/risk/gross-cap.ts`); the cycle asks it after the cash gate and refuses the entry `gross_cap` when it would take gross, held plus resting, past equity. Cash is checked first, so a long-only book still reads `insufficient_cash`.
- **+30% gap bound (T11).** Every CFD short, UK or US, is sized so a +30% gap against it costs at most ~£45 (30% of Q8's ~£150, matching the 2026-09-27 sleeve-share caps): shares <= budget / (0.30 x price in GBP). The ruling states a figure and D8 forbids a capital literal, so the budget is the ruling's arithmetic on the yearly config: 10% of the debate sleeve's share of the year's loss cap (£450 today, so £45). David confirmed the fraction form on 2026-09-29 (#1849). It applies on top of the fixed-risk size; the smaller share count wins.
- **Short availability (T12).** No CFD short, UK or US, when the instrument's Saxo reference data says `ShortTradeDisabled`, when its `CfdBorrowingCost` exceeds 2% a year (ruled for UK names, and on 2026-09-29 confirmed by David for US CFD shorts too), or when no instrument catalogue is loaded. Fail closed: no catalogue, no short. The router checks, in order and failing closed: the CFD entry gates (below), `no_catalogue` (missing, or older than 3 calendar days), `not_in_catalogue`, `asset_type_mismatch` (a US stock needs a `CfdOnStock` row and a UK ETF a `CfdOnEtf` row, so an index row never matches), `not_tradable`, `currency_mismatch`, `ShortTradeDisabled`, `borrow_cost_unknown` (the field is absent or null; a present 0 is a known free borrow), `borrow_cost`. A catalogue with a duplicate `symbol` does not parse. The risk gate refuses a short on a non-CFD venue as `short_requires_cfd` and a long on a CFD venue as `long_on_cfd`, whatever the router did. A simulated CFD fill with no fee or spread model (#1850) is not priced: the cycle journals a `CFD_COST_MODEL` refusal and leaves the order or position open. A refused side becomes a `skip` decision whose `reason` in `v2_decisions` is `short_unavailable:<rule>` (or `long_unavailable:<rule>`), and the home venue stays on the row. The catalogue is a snapshot of Saxo reference data read from `data/saxo-cfd-catalogue.json` <!-- cite-exempt: untracked — gitignored local snapshot --> (`server/apps/v2/data/cfd-catalogue.ts`), never an order endpoint. Its shape is `{ asOf: 'YYYY-MM-DD', instruments: [{ symbol, saxoSymbol, uic, assetType, currency, priceToContractFactor, tradable, shortTradeDisabled, borrowCostPerDay }] }`; `assetType` is `CfdOnStock`, `CfdOnIndex` or `CfdOnEtf`, `currency` is `GBP` or `USD` only (Saxo's GBX quote is normalised to GBP by the refresh, which records `priceToContractFactor` 0.01), and `borrowCostPerDay` is Saxo's `CfdBorrowingCost`, a fraction per day, null when Saxo gives none. Its day count depends on the currency, measured live on 2026-09-30: a USD row is the annual rate / 360 (0.0000138889 is 0.5% a year), a GBP row the annual rate / 365 (ISF's 0.0000547945 is 2%). `borrowCostPerYear` annualises on that basis, and the router compares the annual rate with the ceiling in whole billionths, so a name quoted at exactly 2% passes whichever way the float lands. A file that does not parse is logged (`v2_cfd_catalogue_unreadable`) and treated as no catalogue. The read-only refresh job ([#1916](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1916) part 1, `server/apps/v2/cfd-catalogue-refresh.ts`) writes it: `npm run v2:cfd-catalogue`, and a third leg of the bar refresh on every non-dry run, which logs `v2_cfd_catalogue_refresh_failed` and keeps the last file on any failure. It calls only three GETs: the instrument search (`CfdOnStock` on NYSE and NASDAQ, `CfdOnEtf` on LSE_ETF), the instrument details list (`IsTradable`, `ShortTradeDisabled`, `PriceToContractFactor`, currency) and `infoprices/list` with `InstrumentPriceDetails` (`CfdBorrowingCost`, `ShortTradeDisabled`). Rows are keyed on the universe symbol (the S&P constituents and the `LSE_LINES` tidms; Saxo's `BRKb` is written `BRK.B`), and only listings on those exchanges in the scope's currency count, so Tesco's `TSCO:xlon` never keys to Tractor Supply's `TSCO`; a symbol two rows still key to is dropped and logged `v2_cfd_catalogue_symbol_clash`. `ShortTradeDisabled` is written true when either source says true or neither sends it; a snapshot with no rows, or one `parseCfdCatalogue` rejects, is not written. The entry gates are checked before `no_catalogue`, so while they are unset the refusal a skip journals is still `cfd_*_unset` or `cfd_resting_stop_unverified`. David wants the borrow and `ShortTradeDisabled` fields read live from Saxo each cycle in the end (2026-09-29, #1849); this build keeps the file loader and the 3-day age (T14) as the seam, and #1916 delivers the live read behind it.
- **CFD entry gates.** Five parameters in `server/apps/v2/signal/parameters.ts` (`CFD_ENTRY_GATES`) each block every CFD entry, in the router and again in the risk gate, while unset; `cfdEntryRefusal()` names the first one missing and the cycle journals it every cycle beside the other unset parameters. David ruled cost-model option B on 2026-09-29 (#1849): `CFD_COST_MODEL` keeps its `fee()` seam (`cfd_cost_model_unset`), and `CFD_SPREAD_MODEL` (`cfd_spread_model_unset`), `CFD_FINANCING_MODEL` (`cfd_financing_model_unset`) and `CFD_BORROW_MODEL` (`cfd_borrow_model_unset`) are separate typed seams, all four owned by #1850 (C3 to C6, §5), which set them from Saxo's sourced tariff on 2026-09-30, so only `CFD_RESTING_STOP_VERIFIED` still refuses. `CFD_RESTING_STOP_VERIFIED` (`cfd_resting_stop_unverified`, #1916) stays unset, or false, until a real Saxo resting stop on a CFD is verified: David ruled on 2026-09-29 that there is no CFD paper order, simulated included, before then. Nothing prices a CFD fill with the cash-ETF tariff.
- **Prices.** UK CFDs quote in pence (GBX, `PriceToContractFactor` 0.01, measured on VOD, `LSE_SETS`, 2026-09-29); the bars in the parquet store are already in pounds (`samplesToBars`), so everything inside the sleeve reads pounds and no v2 code converts GBX again; the v1 adapter's `venueOrderPrice` converts back at the order boundary, which stays behind [#1916](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1916). Whole shares only (`AssetType` `CfdOnStock`).
- **Stops.** Postmortem §3 binds: a CFD position needs a broker-resting stop. Saxo's CFD order types are Market, Limit, StopIfTraded, TrailingStopIfTraded and StopLimit, and OCO is not supported (`IsOcoOrderSupported` false), so a bracket is a stop plus a limit as related orders that the adapter must cancel on fill itself. That path is unverified against the live venue (no precheck was allowed) and stays behind the live gates (#895, #900); paper runs simulate: `V2OrderExecutor.simulates()` is true for every venue but `alpaca`, so no CFD order reaches a broker adapter. #1849's kill line is that before the first CFD paper order every CFD position rests a stop at Saxo that survives the process exiting; this build does not meet it, because a simulated CFD position has only the simulated stop the cycle evaluates on the next bar. What holds the line is `CFD_RESTING_STOP_VERIFIED`, unset until that stop is verified (David, 2026-09-29: no CFD paper order, simulated included, before then), on top of the unset cost models (#1850). The real-adapter work is [#1916](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1916).
- **Universe.** The UK single-stock CFD leg adds no names until [#1866](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1866); the pool and screens are [#1914](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1914). This ticket builds the routes, not the pool.

Small caps (G18 2): long-only, half a large-cap trade's risk, capped at a fixed share of the sleeve, a counted trial vs a large-cap-only shadow. The liquidity, price and market-cap floors and the cap are **unset — Needs David** (`G18_SMALL_CAP_FLOORS`, #1753); until set the pool is large caps only, journalled every cycle as a `universe` refusal, and the `large-cap-only` shadow is not instantiated (§7).

## 3. Inputs

- Daily bars (the local Parquet store, venue `alpaca` under `data/bars/parquet/`, for the dry run; the same reader serves the paper run once a forward puller lands). <!-- cite-exempt: untracked — local bar store, gitignored since #1929 -->
- Technical read: close vs 200-day SMA, 20- and 63-day trailing return, 20-day ATR, and — per doc 66's 2026-09-25 candlestick ruling (#1772) — the prior daily bar's body and upper/lower wick as fractions of its high-low range, `undefined`/`n/a` on a zero-or-negative range or a shape defect (open or close outside the bar's own high-low — 0.45%/1.4% of Alpaca/Saxo bars over each symbol's last 250 sessions, 0.79%/7.7% over full history, per #1838) — one `AnalystView` per name.
- News: Alpaca News (Benzinga) keyed on the US symbol (doc 69 R6), wired in `server/apps/v2/data/news.ts` over the existing `AlpacaNewsClient` (`server/providers/market-intelligence/sources/alpaca-news-client.ts`, free on the Alpaca keys). Window: from 00:00Z of the calendar day before the entry session to now; articles tagged with more than 5 symbols are class-wide roundups and are dropped (G18 3); the last 10 headlines per name become one neutral `news` `AnalystView` (`newsView`) next to the technical view, so both debaters and the judge read them, and the headlines are part of the decision's `inputs_hash`. A news fetch failure skips the name (`news_error:…`) rather than debating on stale inputs. Each US lookup is journalled to `v2_news` (provider `alpaca`) when the fetch returns and before any prompt is built: `ok` with the kept headlines (title, publish time and Alpaca article id), `no_news` when none survive the filters, or `error` with the credential-masked failure text, the same text the decision's `news_error:` reason carries (#1981). Keys and request headers are never stored, and neither are article bodies or URLs as fields; but a malformed-article error quotes up to 200 characters of the raw article JSON, as `AlpacaNewsClient` reports it, in that reason (and so in `v2_decisions.reason`). The two news paths treat a failed `v2_news` write differently: on the Alpaca path a failed write after a successful fetch fails the lookup, so the name skips as `news_error:` (fail closed, like any Alpaca news failure), while a failed write after a failed fetch is dropped so the fetch's own error stays the reason; on the Marketaux path any failure, a ledger write included, degrades the name to NO_NEWS, as every Marketaux failure does. The dry run uses `NO_NEWS` (no keys); the paper run needs `ALPACA_API_KEY`/`ALPACA_API_SECRET`, which it already needs for the broker. UK single stocks (once the pool, #1914, supplies the `isUkStock` predicate) read Marketaux instead (#1915, `server/apps/v2/data/marketaux-news.ts`, doc 75): the free key in `MARKETAUX_API_KEY`, window from 00:00Z three calendar days before the entry session to now, hard stop at 80 requests per UTC day, one lookup per name per trading day cached in `v2_news`, only articles published before `now`, and NO_NEWS with a journalled reason when the key is unset, the budget is spent or the request fails (a Marketaux failure never skips the name, unlike the Alpaca path). Each cycle logs how many UK names had headlines against NO_NEWS. UK ETFs stay NO_NEWS (G18 2).
- Sentiment and social (G18 1): each a counted trial. Social source is **unset — Needs David** (`G18_SOCIAL_SOURCE`, #1753). Sentiment reuses the v1 analyst once its duplication rule is set — **unset** (`G18_SENTIMENT_DEDUP_RULE`, #1753, where the constraint is ruled: one underlying view votes once, and a market-wide roundup tagging more than 5 symbols is not scored per name). Both are journalled to `v2_refusals` every cycle (scope `parameter`, with the other unset cycle-level parameters `ALPACA_SHORT_EQUITY_FLOOR_USD`, `CFD_COST_MODEL`, `CFD_SPREAD_MODEL`, `CFD_FINANCING_MODEL`, `CFD_BORROW_MODEL` and `CFD_RESTING_STOP_VERIFIED`); the `no-sentiment` and `no-social` shadows are not instantiated until the input they remove exists (§7).
- No account data or key ever enters an LLM request: prompts carry analyst views only (`renderMessageContent` in the debate core wraps context as untrusted text).

The ids, `canonical_slug`s and rates above were read from `GET {NOUS_BASE_URL}/models` on 2026-09-25, the judge row on 2026-09-27, when 10 of 10 fetches gave its dated slug and five realistic judge prompts each echoed the id, finished `stop` within 937 output tokens and parsed as the mediator JSON (raw listing kept in the session scratch as `nous-models.json`), and each id answered an 8-token `POST /chat/completions` probe echoing its own id as `model`. Nous echoes the bare requested id, not the dated `canonical_slug` — confirmed again by the evaluator's four 64-token calls on 2026-09-25 — so the echo check compares against the Nous id in the table, not the slug. `server/shared/llm/pricing.ts` carries these four rows at the catalogue rate; the pin's `priced` id is its Nous id, so `llm_spend` rows and the pricing table key on the same string. Nous has no OpenRouter-style `provider` routing field, so the earlier `allow_fallbacks: false` / `data_collection: 'deny'` pins do not apply and have no substitute; the guards against a silent swap are the startup slug check below and the response check: a reply whose echoed `model` differs from the pin's Nous id is refused (`LlmProviderError`, `NousPinnedTransport` in `server/apps/v2/signal/llm-transport.ts`, over `NousMessagesClient`) and the debate for that name is journalled as `llm_error`, so a silent provider swap cannot produce a decision; the tokens Nous billed for that refused reply — and for a truncated or refused one — still land in `llm_spend` under the pin's priced id before the error propagates (`AnthropicLlmClient` records the usage a typed error carries), so the monthly cap counts failures too; a reply that carries no `model` field at all passes the guard (accepted: Nous echoes it in practice, and the log line shows `unreported`). Every successful reply's echoed `model` is logged (`v2_llm_upstream_model`); a truncated or refused reply throws before that line. The echo alone cannot see a snapshot swap behind an undated id, so before any debate the paper run (`main` in `server/apps/v2/index.ts`) reads `GET {NOUS_BASE_URL}/models` and refuses to start unless every pin's id is listed with the `canonical_slug` in the table above (`verifyNousPins` in `server/apps/v2/signal/nous-pin-check.ts`; each pin carries its slug as `canonicalSlug`); a failed request, a non-2xx answer or an unreadable listing refuses the run too, and a pass logs each pin's observed slug and whether it was checked (`v2_llm_pins_verified`). Sonnet 5 is the exception, ruled by David on 2026-09-25 (*"Keep undated, accept gap"*, doc 66, #1787): Nous lists no dated Sonnet 5 id, and its `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5` row carries the undated slug in some fetches and `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5-20260630` in others (4 of 18 on 2026-09-25). The Sonnet pin therefore has no `canonicalSlug`: the id must still be listed, any slug is accepted and logged, and a Sonnet snapshot swap is not detected. The other three seats stay strict.

Roles rotate daily (Q16): the three debater providers rotate through the debate core's two debater seats (bull, bear) by day index — each provider sits out every third day and the bull/bear assignment flips every three days, so a provider argues the same side at most twice in a row (`rotateSeats` in `server/apps/v2/signal/llm-panel.ts`); the seat models for the day are part of the decision's `inputs_hash`. The core (`runDebate`) has two debater seats and one mediator; a third simultaneous seat is follow-on work, recorded here rather than bolted onto the core.

In-flight cap: one call at a time for the whole Nous account, shared by all four seats (one `NousAccountInFlightGate({ maxInFlight: 1 })`, per the per-account queue finding — the queue is per account, not per key or per model). Spend: `SqliteMonthlySpendCap` over `llm_spend`, $30 per calendar month (UTC), checked before every debate (so the overshoot is bounded by one debate's three calls), fail-closed on a read fault — a breach stops LLM calls, never exits.

## 5. Decision, sizing and the pre-declared trial register

- Direction: the judge's verdict (`DebateResult.direction`). Neutral = no entry. The judge's JSON carries no confidence, so the journalled confidence is a pre-declared mapping (trial T5): 0.5 when neither debater agrees with the judge, 0.75 when one does, 1.0 when both do (`JUDGE_CONFIDENCE_BY_AGREEING_DEBATERS` in `server/apps/v2/signal/debate-sleeve.ts`). It is journalled, never used for sizing or entry, so a different mapping changes rows, not trades.
- Shorts: allowed by Q17/Q8 and routed to Saxo CFDs (Venue routes, §2), which replaces the `SHORTS_ENABLED = false` flag and the Alpaca $2,000 floor route (`ALPACA_SHORT_EQUITY_FLOOR_USD` stays unset and is now moot: no short goes to Alpaca). A bearish verdict opens a short only when its CFD route is available: a catalogue is loaded, the instrument is not `ShortTradeDisabled`, its borrow cost is at most 2% a year, and all five CFD entry gates are set (`CFD_COST_MODEL`, `CFD_SPREAD_MODEL`, `CFD_FINANCING_MODEL`, `CFD_BORROW_MODEL`, `CFD_RESTING_STOP_VERIFIED`). Otherwise the name is a `skip` with reason `short_unavailable:<rule>`. A short's stop is price + 2 × ATR and its target price − 3 × ATR (T2, T3 mirrored), and its size is the smaller of the fixed-risk size and the +30% gap bound (§2).
- Exits (ADR §5 item 9), all pre-declared here and counted: a resting stop at 2 × ATR(20) (trial T2, `DEBATE_STOP_ATR_MULTIPLE = 2`, the same stop doc 70 §2.9 pre-declared for momentum); a bracket target at 3 × ATR(20) (trial T3, `DEBATE_TARGET_ATR_MULTIPLE = 3`: 1.5R against the 2-ATR stop, so the trade pays at 40% accuracy before costs, and `submitBracket` cannot express a stop-only entry, so a target is the shape the venue path already supports); a time stop after 10 marked sessions (trial T4, `DEBATE_TIME_STOP_TRADING_DAYS = 10`, the "e.g. 10 days" doc 66 Q9 wrote down for the swing hold), submitted as a flatten through the book's broker at the 11th cycle. Every leg is `gtc`, never `day`.
- Size: fixed fractional risk at the stop distance, **pre-declared at 0.5% of book equity per trade (trial T0, `DEBATE_RISK_FRACTION = 0.005`).** Source: G6's £1,500/yr loss budget and 1.0% daily cap, applied through debate's 30% capital share (doc 66, 2026-09-27 "Capital and caps by sleeve share" ruling, [#1779](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1779)): on a £2,000 paper start the debate book holds £600, its daily cap is £6 and its −£150/−£300/−£450 loss-budget steps are 30% of the account's £500/£1,000/£1,500 — at 0.5% a day's two full stop-outs (£3 each) sit exactly on the £6 daily cap, and the −£150 half-size step is 50 consecutive full stop-outs away, which is the loosest fraction that keeps one bad day inside G6; G18 (2) then fixes small caps at half of it (0.25%). ## 7. Books, fills and exits (Q14, G5, G16, G18)

Each instantiated book is a paper book seeded at its sleeve's share of the year's capital config's start capital (`sleeveAllocationGbp`, `server/apps/v2/risk/allocation.ts`; `v2_capital_config`, set with `npm run v2:capital`; paper is £2,000 from 2026-09-25, doc 66 D8, so debate's book seeds at £600, its 30% capital share; no literal in code). No book opens, and no entry is sized, before a config is in force for the date. Each book has its own cash (`v2_books.cash_gbp`), positions (`v2_positions`) and `LossBudget`, rebuilt from `v2_book_days` on every start so a halt and the 1 January reference equity (ruling (j)) survive the once-a-day process. A shadow book runs the same decisions with one input removed; a shadow that would be a byte-identical copy of the primary is **not instantiated**, because it would burn LLM spend and prove nothing:

|---|---|---|---|
| `debate/primary` | debate | — | instantiated |
| `debate/no-macro-gate` | debate | no half-size on macro days (G16) | instantiated |
| `debate/no-sentiment` | debate | sentiment input off (G18) | declared, not instantiated: there is no sentiment input to remove until `G18_SENTIMENT_DEDUP_RULE` is set (#1753) |
| `debate/no-social` | debate | social input off (G18) | declared, not instantiated: no social input until `G18_SOCIAL_SOURCE` is set (#1753) |
| `debate/large-cap-only` | debate | small caps excluded (G18) | declared, not instantiated: the pool is large-cap only until `G18_SMALL_CAP_FLOORS` is set (#1753) |

`DEBATE_SLEEVE_SPEC.books` in `server/apps/v2/signal/parameters.ts` carries all five with an `instantiated` flag; flipping a flag is the whole change once the input exists. The G5 `no-veto` shadow is a variant any sleeve can declare (Step 3d); the debate sleeve sets no `veto`, so it declares none. `arm2/technical-only` is arm 2's own book, not a debate shadow — it never routes a real order (`V2OrderExecutor.simulates()` treats every non-`primary` variant as always-simulated), so `ARM2_SLEEVE_SPEC.capitalShare` sizes and seeds its book (Q14) but is excluded from the account-wide capital-share ceiling `assertCapitalShares` enforces across sleeves that can hold a real fill (doc 66, 2026-09-28 addition; `server/apps/v2/risk/allocation.ts`).

**Brokers per book.** The primary submits to the real Alpaca paper account (`AlpacaBrokerAdapter` over `AlpacaHttpBrokerClient`, bracket state in `broker_brackets` via `SqliteBrokerStateStore` so a resting bracket is rehydrated by the next day's process). Every shadow, and the primary in a dry run, submits to the `DryRunBrokerAdapter` for its venue, which refuses the submission (so nothing rests anywhere) and never reports a fill; simulated entries and flattens are filled by a later cycle from the journal (§7, Simulated entries and Simulated flattens). Fills are routed to books by their deterministic client order id (`v2-<book>-<date>-<instrument>[-exit]`), journalled to `v2_fills` (idempotent on the broker fill id) and applied to cash and positions; so both a real fill and a simulated one move a book the same way, and the loss budget, the daily cap and the custody accrual move with them (tested: a losing fill sequence steps the primary to half size at −£150, quarter at −£300, halt at −£450 — debate's 30% share of the account's −£500/−£1,000/−£1,500 steps).

**Marks.** Each cycle marks every book at the last daily bar strictly before the entry session (or at the entry price if no bar); a position marked from a bar more than 5 calendar days old, or from its entry price, is journalled as a `MARK_FRESHNESS` data refusal naming the book, instrument and price used ([#1804](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1804)), so a stale mark in the loss budget is visible, not silent. The mark runs after the day's submissions, so a crash between submit and mark leaves the date unmarked and the retry idempotent (an entry whose order id is already journalled, or whose name is already held, is not resubmitted). A date any book has already marked is skipped with a `cycle` refusal row, not re-run. The cancels a blocking mark triggers (§7, Exits) run after the mark is written, so a crash between them is repaired at the start of the next cycle: before any simulated entry fills, every book whose last mark halted it or tripped the daily cap has its resting entries cancelled.

**Exits.** Real broker (primary in paper mode): the stop and target rest at Alpaca as bracket legs, their fills arrive through `fetchNewFills` on later cycles; the time stop submits a flatten (`submitFlatten`) once a position has been marked `DEBATE_TIME_STOP_TRADING_DAYS` times, the pending exit id is stored on the position and `resumeFlatten` is called each cycle until its fill arrives. Simulated books: the bracket is evaluated on the prior daily bar each cycle, its high and low rescaled from dividend-adjusted to quoted prices (× raw close ÷ close, since the stop and target are set from quoted prices) — stop first (conservative: a bar touching both legs is a stop-out), then target. A stop leg is a market order once triggered, so it fills at the stop moved against the order by half the spread plus impact, and pays the fee; a target leg is a resting limit and fills at the target with the fee only. The time stop and a halt send a flatten that fills at a later cycle (Simulated flattens below). An entry submitted to Alpaca that has not filled by the next cycle is cancelled and journalled `cancelled`. A mark that halts the book or trips the daily cap also cancels every entry of that book still resting, real or simulated, so nothing decided before the block fills after it (doc 66, 2026-09-27, [#1813](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1813)); each cancelling mark records one `LOSS_BUDGET` refusal naming the count, and a cancel the venue refuses is logged and left resting. Nothing in Step 3 sits above an exit: refusals gate entries only.

**Simulated entries** ([#1797](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1797)). A simulated book models the order the paper primary sends: a GTC marketable limit, cancelled by the next cycle if unfilled. David ruled on 2026-09-29 (#1815): the reference is the decision close, and the limit sits 50 bps through it, so a buy rests at close × 1.005 and a short at close × 0.995 (`ENTRY_LIMIT_OFFSET`, `server/apps/v2/risk/entry-limit.ts`). The 50 bps is a judgement, not a measurement; doc 72 measured 0 bps best over 745 US names. Changing either value is a new counted trial. The risk gate sets the limit and every book uses it: the Alpaca order, the simulated fill below, the cash gate and the sizing. The stop and the target stay where the sleeve set them from the close. An entry whose limit reaches its target is refused `offset_past_target`, and one whose limit reaches its stop `offset_past_stop`. The limit is the same on every venue, the Saxo LSE leg included (David, 2026-09-30). The offset is part of the backtest trial identity (`entryOffsetIdentity`, `server/apps/v2/backtest.ts`), so a candidate re-run at another offset is a new counted trial; a 0 bps run keeps the hash of the trials recorded before the offset joined it. The journal keeps the decision close as the entry's `price`, the order's `limit` beside it, and the offset it used as `entry_offset_bps` (absent for a sleeve's own limit). The paper sample splits at the change: an entry journalled without a `limit` went out at 0 bps, and the Evidence trade count (`server/apps/v2/api/evidence.ts`) tags each closed trade with its entry's offset and counts only the current offset and sleeve-set limits toward G1's 100. `npm run v2:entry-offsets` (`server/apps/v2/report-entry-offsets.ts`) replays from the close, scoring the debate's own entries at 0/50/100/200 bps and at the open over the 10-bar hold, net of SPY, so the offset is re-checked on paper. The signals sleeve sends its own limit (`entry_limit`, from `server/apps/v2/signals/processor.ts`) and gets no offset. Each cycle, before any sleeve decides, it takes every simulated or dry-run entry from an earlier date with no fill (`unfilledSimulatedEntriesBefore`) and reads that name's bars from the entry's trading date up to the day before the cycle, rescaled to quoted prices as above (`server/apps/v2/simulated-entry.ts`). A buy fills on the first bar whose low reaches the limit, at the lower of the open and the limit; a short sale on the first bar whose high reaches it, at the higher of the two. A fill at the limit is passive (no spread or impact); a fill at the open crossed the spread. The stop is live from the fill: if the fill bar also reaches it, the position exits the same bar at the worse of stop and fill (`stop_on_entry_bar`, across the spread); the target is not checked on the fill bar, and the next cycle's bracket check starts from the following bar. Bars exist and none reached the limit: the entry is journalled `cancelled`, as the real order is. No bar yet: the entry stays pending and a second entry on the same book and name is refused meanwhile; pending more than 5 calendar days, it is cancelled. The position opens on the cycle that fills it, so it is marked from that day.

**Simulated flattens** ([#1812](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1812)). A time stop or halt flatten on a simulated book models the market order the paper primary sends pre-open. The exit id is stored on the position, as for paper. Each cycle, before any sleeve decides, every position whose exit order was simulated or refused in a dry run is filled at the open of the first bar on or after the flatten's trading date, rescaled to quoted prices, across the spread with impact and fee (`simulateMarketExit`). Bars dated before the flatten are ignored, and bars after the first are not used, however many cycles were missed. No bar yet: the flatten stays pending, with no expiry, because a market order does not lapse. While a flatten is pending, the bracket is not checked (the open comes before any intraday stop or target) and no second flatten is sent.

**Cost fidelity** ([#1884](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1884)). `npm run v2:cost-fidelity [store] [bar root] [from] [to] [paper|live]` (`server/apps/v2/report-cost-fidelity.ts`, `server/apps/v2/cost-fidelity.ts`) reads the journal read-only and replays every order that reached Alpaca (a primary book, not a dry run) through the simulator above: an entry through `simulateLimitEntry` over the bars from its trading date up to the cycle that booked the fill or cancelled it, a stop or target leg through the daily-bar bracket check on its session bar (the last bar before the booking cycle), a flatten at the open. Fidelity per order leg: `match` when the simulator fills it on the same bar, `bar_mismatch` on another bar, `broker_only` when the simulator does not fill it, `sim_only` for a cancelled entry the simulator would have filled, `both_unfilled`, or `pending` with no bar yet. For each `match`, realised cost is the broker fill (fill parts summed to a VWAP) against the simulator's cost-free price on that bar (the open, the limit or the stop), not the decision close, so the overnight gap is not cost; modelled slippage is the half spread plus impact the cycle journalled as `modelled_slippage_bps` in the order's payload when the order went out (a broker entry's, which its stop and target legs share, and a flatten's), charged on a leg that crosses the spread and capped at the limit as a simulated entry is; an order journalled before that is priced from today's half-spread table and the booking cycle's bars. Modelled fee is the fee schedule on the same price for the same quantity. Q19's check is aggregate: Σ realised ÷ Σ modelled within ±25% inclusive passes, and no modelled cost is `insufficient`. On paper the check compares slippage only, because Alpaca paper charges no fee while the model charges SEC, TAF and CAT fees; on live the broker and modelled fees join both sums (doc 66, #1884, 2026-10-01). The fee legs are printed apart either way. The default window is the whole journal. The verdict is split by entry offset (doc 66, #1815, 2026-09-30); an exit carries the offset of the entry it closes: a bracket leg's own order, any other exit the latest entry filled before it on its book and name. The closed-trade count on the evidence panel reads the same rule (`server/apps/v2/entry-offset.ts`).

**Deferred to doc 67 Step 4** (exactly): (1) comparability — the primary's Alpaca fills are intraday limit/stop fills while shadows fill on daily bars, so primary-vs-shadow differences carry an execution term the shadow cannot see (in particular a simulated entry's target is not checked on its fill bar while Alpaca's target leg is live from the fill, and a simulated entry with no bar yet stays pending up to 5 calendar days while the paper order is cancelled at the next cycle); a same-bar simulated twin of the primary is Step 4's fix; (2) partial fills — since #1873 a `qty_is_cumulative` report is booked as the increment over what the journal already holds for that broker order (a duplicate adds nothing, a report below the booked quantity is ignored with a warning), but Alpaca holds a bracket's legs until the entry fills completely and cancels them with the parent, so a partially filled entry has no resting stop, and `unfilledEntriesBefore`/`restingEntries` treat any fill as filled, so a partial GTC remainder is never cancelled; (3) a position whose Alpaca bracket legs were cancelled outside the system (the adapter's rearm path exists but is not called from the cycle); (4) the Saxo paper adapter — built in the step that first routes an LSE order, with the tariff constants already in `server/pipeline/momentum/costs.ts`; the CFD routes are simulated only (§2, §8), and the resting-stop path for a CFD at the real venue stays unbuilt and unverified behind #895 and #900.

**Arm 2** (doc 71 §6, built #1773): a separate sleeve (`createArm2Sleeve`, `server/apps/v2/signal/arm2-sleeve.ts`), not a `BookVariant` of the debate sleeve, sharing the same universe, clock, exits and fixed risk via extraction (`resolveTechnical`, `buildUniverse`, `actionFor`, `stopFor`, `skipped`, `server/apps/v2/signal/debate-sleeve.ts`) rather than duplication. Entry is the technical analyst's own axis vote — long when close > 200-day SMA and the 63-day return is positive, short when the mirror holds (a short goes to a CFD route only when available, §2) — at David-approved thresholds `{ longAbove: 0, shortBelow: 0 }` (T9, `ARM2_ENTRY_THRESHOLDS`), reproducing the debate technical view's own zero-threshold read exactly. It makes no LLM call and reads no headline (`Arm2SleeveDeps` carries no `panel`, no `news`); its own confidence (0.5 neutral, 0.6 otherwise) is derived from its own `direction`, not borrowed from the debate technical view, so a future threshold change cannot desync it. `assertArm2RunsBesideDebate` (`server/apps/v2/risk/allocation.ts`) refuses `composeV2Root` if exactly one of the debate sleeve and arm 2 is registered: no debate-sleeve paper trade runs without arm 2 beside it.

## 9. Journal and replay

`v2_decisions` (one row per name per book per day with the input hash, verdict, size, stop, reason), `v2_orders` (every order — entry or exit, real, simulated, refused, rejected or cancelled — with its book, leg, side, `dry_run` flag, outcome and payload), `v2_fills` (every fill, real or simulated, keyed by venue and broker fill id, with the broker's own fill time as `filled_at` since #1983; null on a simulated or cash-in-lieu fill and on a row from before it), `v2_rescales` (#1983: one append-only row per change the cycle makes to a held position's split units, written in the same transaction as the change: trading date, book, name, source, ratio, anchor date, the qty, entry price, stop and target before and after, and how many `v2_fills` rows of that trading date the journal held when it was written. The source is `detector` for a split read off the bars, `broker` for a ledger rescaled to a broker fill's post-split units, `entry` for a simulated entry booked into the latest bar's units, and `anchor` for a broker entry anchored at its fill date, ratio 1), `v2_positions` (open positions per book with stop, target, pending exit id, marks held, and the split factor and anchor date the cycle rescales a held position by when the bars' adjustment factor steps across a split, #1865), `v2_book_days` (equity, cash, invested, YTD loss, size multiplier, custody accrual, CFD financing accrual, CFD borrow accrual, recorded-at), `v2_refusals` (every unset-parameter, fail-closed or skipped-date refusal), `v2_news` (one append-only row per news lookup, keyed by provider: status, reason, whether a request was spent, the provider's found count, and the headlines with publish times; for UK names (`marketaux`) it is the per-day cache, the request-budget ledger and the record of UK headlines, #1915; for US names (`alpaca`) it is the record of the headlines each debate read, with each article's id, #1981; the cache and the UK coverage count read only `marketaux` rows). The `v2` store handle is the sole writer of these eight tables (`server/shared/store/write-guard.ts`). `v2_input_digests` (#1982, also written only by the `v2` handle) holds, once per day's cycle, a digest of the inputs the cycle read rather than a copy of them (doc 66, 2026-10-01 ruling 4): for each name whose bars the cycle loaded, the sha256 of the bars dated before the day (date, open, high, low, close, volume, raw close) with the first and last bar date and the row count, and for the CFD catalogue the sha256 of the file's bytes and its `asOf` (both null when no catalogue was read). The root (`runDigestedCycle`, `server/apps/v2/index.ts`; `server/apps/v2/input-digest.ts`) writes the rows after a cycle that ran; a cycle that throws or a marked day that is skipped writes none, and the first cycle's row per name and input is kept (append-only, a later insert of the same key is ignored). Measured on the committed bar store (2026-10-01, a 4-vCPU container): a cycle reads 526 names, 1.40 million bars, and digests them in about 0.3 s against a 6 s dry-run cycle; the insert takes under 10 ms and adds about 80 KB a day. LLM calls go to `llm_spend` and, with text capture on, `llm_call_log` (prompt and completion per call) with the pin's priced id. Every attempt is a row, retries included (#1980): an answered call records its `stop_reason`; a failed call, billed or not, records `error_class` (the thrown error's class, a timeout with its source) and `error_message` (masked and capped like the response) and no response, and a failure with no billed usage, or an answer with no usage block, is metered at zero cost. No request header, URL or key is written. Before a record reaches the store, the #1881 secret guard checks its prompt, response, stop reason, error class and error message for any known secret value (`secretGuardedSink`, `server/apps/v2/signal/secret-guard.ts`). A record that carries one keeps its metering but loses all its text, and its `error_class` reads `secret_withheld`. That is how a request the egress guard refused before send is logged. Replay: the decision row carries `inputs_hash` over the 200 bars read, the technical and news views and the day's seat models; re-running a day with the same bars, headlines and the journalled completions reproduces the same rows. Since #1880, `npm run v2:replay -- --date YYYY-MM-DD [--store path] [--bars root] [--constituents path] [--fx path] [--cfd-catalogue path] [--spreads path] [--saxo-spreads path]` (`server/apps/v2/replay.ts`, `server/apps/v2/replay-cli.ts`) does this for the debate and arm 2 decisions. It opens the store read-only (the paper store by default) and builds the sleeves the root builds (`decisionSleeves`, `server/apps/v2/index.ts`) from the bar store, constituents file and CFD catalogue it is given. The same cycle config applies: the code's parameters, and the day's `SAXO_SESSION` refusal read back as the LSE leg refusal. Every LLM request is answered from that day's `llm_call_log` rows through `ReplayTransport` (`server/apps/v2/signal/replay-transport.ts`). A request matches a row on the served model and the prompt in the form the spend sink logs it (masked, capped at 16,384 characters). The view timestamps are normalised first: they are wall-clock reads, and `inputs_hash` and the debate id leave them out too. Identical repeated requests, such as a retry after a malformed answer, take the logged rows in order. No model is called. A logged failure is replayed as the same error class with the logged message, so the retry and the decision reason follow the original path. A class the replay does not rebuild, such as a cancellation or a gate refusal, replays as a provider error with the same message, which is equally not retried. An answered call replays with its logged `stop_reason`, so a refusal replays as a refusal; a row from before #1980 has none and replays as `end_turn`. A request with no row, a row with no captured response and no error, a withheld row, or a response that a capture cap truncated (16,384 characters, or 4,096 for a row from before #1980) fails that request and the replay. Headlines are served from the name's first `v2_news` row for that date (`journalledNewsSource`, `server/apps/v2/data/journalled-news.ts`, #1981), which is the lookup the day's first debate of that name read. A US (`alpaca`) row returns its headlines in journalled order, and an `error` row fails the name with its journalled reason, as the cycle did. A UK (`marketaux`) row returns its headlines published before the row's own `fetched_at`, the clock the live read filtered them by, and a failed or refused UK lookup replays as NO_NEWS, as it ran. A name with no row (a day before #1981, or a UK lookup whose own ledger write failed) falls back to the news view read back from the name's first logged prompt; for those the news part of `inputs_hash` checks consistency with what the models saw, not an independent record. The spend cap is answered from the journal too (`journalledSpendCap`, `server/apps/v2/replay-spend-cap.ts`, #1987): the sleeve checks it once per name, after hashing the inputs, and passes the name, so each check gets that name's verdict: a name with a debate row whose reason is `llm_spend_cap:<kind>` replays as that refusal, every other name as admitted. The verdict is keyed by name, not by journal order, because a book journals its sit-outs before its other decisions. The verdicts are read from the store the replay was given, not the in-memory copy, which empties `llm_spend` and drops the day's decision rows. Each journalled row of a debate or arm 2 book is compared with the replayed decision after the book's veto: venue, `inputs_hash`, direction, confidence, action, reason, stop, and the payload bytes, debate id included. Since #1977 the replay also runs the day's trading through the cycle itself (`runCycle` over `composeCycle`), so sizing, the risk gate, the orders, the fills and the marks are re-run by the same code (`server/apps/v2/replay-book.ts`, `server/apps/v2/replay-broker.ts`). The store is never written: the cycle runs against an in-memory copy, which is cut back to the morning of the day. Every row dated on or after the day is deleted from the copy, as is every control set after the day's first journal row. An order cancelled on or after the day is put back to the outcome it rested under (from its route, as the executor decides it). The books are then rebuilt from the journal: cash and positions from every earlier fill and every earlier `v2_rescales` row in journal order (a rescale lands after the number of that day's fills it records and before the next), each mark's custody and carry accrual and its `marks_held` step (time stops paused as the venue calendar pauses them), and a pending flatten from the latest exit order since the position opened that has neither a fill nor a rearm. The loss budget is rebuilt from `v2_book_days` as every start rebuilds it. No venue adapter is built. A day whose orders were journalled with `dry_run` replays through the dry-run brokers; a day with no orders of its own takes the mode from the rest of the store. Any other day replays the primary's Alpaca leg through `JournalReplayBroker`, which answers from that day's journal: a submission with its journalled outcome and state, each fill at its journalled `filled_at` (its `recorded_at`, the sweep time, for a row from before #1983), a cancel only where the journal cancelled that day, the first fill sweep with that day's broker fills journalled no later than its first reconcile (the cycle reconciles after that sweep; all of them on a day with no reconcile row) and every later sweep with all of them (the native price recovered so that the cycle books the journalled GBP bits), a flatten as cancelled where the day rearmed it, filled where its fills up to the same first-reconcile cut reach its size, partially filled where they fall short and still working where none came that day, and a rearm as the day's rearm order went. The reconcile mirrors the replayed books after a clean journalled reconcile and blocks entries after any other. Whether a run woke past an exchange's entry cutoff is read back from its `late_wake_entry_cutoff` refusal, because the run's start instant is not journalled. Two checks run before the cycle. The rebuilt cash and invested notional must equal the last mark's (`book`), so a changed earlier fill shows there. Each book's last mark is re-marked from its journalled equity alone, and the loss-budget state that gives must equal the journalled `ytd_loss_gbp`, `size_multiplier` and `entries_blocked` (`gate`). After the cycle, `size_shares` (`sizing`), `v2_orders` (`orders`: every column but `recorded_at`, the decision as its book and name, the payload bytes), `v2_rescales` (`rescales`: ratio and the levels before and after, keyed by book, name, source and anchor date), `v2_fills` (`fills`) and `v2_book_days` (`marks`) are compared field by field, byte for byte, with the rows the day journalled up to its last mark; later rows on the same date, such as a signals pass, are left out. The replay also recomputes each journalled digest from the bars and catalogue it was given and reports each one that differs as `input_changed_since` (the name, the input, the journalled and the current digest). A day journalled before #1982 has no digests and skips this check. The report names the first divergence in causal order and exits 1. The order is: an input changed since the day; then a rebuilt book state, then a loss-budget state, that differs; then an `inputs_hash` difference (the bars or headlines changed); then a request the log does not hold, shown with the nearest logged prompt of that model and the offset where they part; then a decision field, or a row journalled but not replayed (or the reverse); then sizing, orders, rescales, fills and marks, in that order; then a logged call the replay never made. A changed bar of a held name that the day did not decide therefore shows first as `input_changed_since` (the cycle reads a held name's bars, so the day's digest covers it), and on a day with no digests at sizing, through the equity the gate sizes from. Known secret values and credential patterns are masked out of what it prints. It prints `identical` and exits 0 only when nothing diverged.

## 10. Dry run

`node dist/server/apps/v2/index.js --dry-run --date YYYY-MM-DD` (built by `npm run build`; no keys). It writes to its own store (`data/samurai-v2-dry-run.sqlite` <!-- cite-exempt: untracked — gitignored runtime database -->, never the paper store). Every book submits to the `DryRunBrokerAdapter`, which refuses every submission (`v2_orders.outcome = 'refused_dry_run'` for the primary, `'simulated'` for the shadow); entries and flattens fill from a later cycle's bars (§7); LLM transports are `ScriptedTransport` with `BULLISH_SCRIPT` (every debater and the judge answer bullish) and every scripted call still lands in `llm_spend` and `llm_call_log`; news is `NO_NEWS`. **2026-09-24 run (Step 3 build, before #1797's lagged entries):** 20 decisions, 2 entries (PAYX, 1 share, in each book), 1 dry-run refusal, 1 simulated order, 0 submitted, 2 fills, 60 LLM calls journalled, 5 needs-David refusals, both books marked at £999.99 with one position each. The exit code is non-zero if any order was submitted. `npm run smoke` also runs `server/apps/v2/smoke.ts`: the same cycle as a probe plus assertions on zero submitted orders, entries reaching the dry-run broker, no fill on the entry day and every entry filled or cancelled on the next day's bar with the fills reaching the books, the registry contents, the pins, the monthly cap, the macro halving, the unset parameters, the declared-not-instantiated shadows, and that a keyless paper run is refused with the `without NOUS_BASE_URL and a Nous key (NOUS_DEBATE_API_KEY or NOUS_API_KEY)` error. The paper run is the same binary without the flag: `node --env-file=.env.local dist/server/apps/v2/index.js --date YYYY-MM-DD` — `main()` reads only the env it is handed, and nothing in the root loads `.env.local` itself, so the `--env-file` flag (as `npm start` uses for v1) is how the Nous variables reach it. Outside a dry run the root refuses `SAMURAI_MODE=live` and refuses to start without non-empty `NOUS_BASE_URL` and a Nous key (`NOUS_DEBATE_API_KEY`, else `NOUS_API_KEY`; both trimmed) before opening any store — a scripted verdict never reaches a broker. A date any book has already marked is skipped with a journal line before any LLM call. A paper run (never a dry run) ends with a healthchecks.io dead-man ping to `HEALTHCHECKS_PING_URL` (doc 66 D1/D5, [#1784](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1784), `server/apps/v2/heartbeat.ts`): success on exit 0, the check's `/fail` endpoint on a non-zero exit or a throw (a bad argument included), a 10 s timeout; a failed or unset ping is a warning and never changes the exit code, and the URL, a secret, is never logged. A missed day raises the alert from outside the Mac, which an in-process Telegram alert cannot. `npm run v2:run -- [--dry-run] [--date YYYY-MM-DD]` runs the same entrypoint; the launchd job `ops/launchd/com.samurai.v2-paper.plist` schedules the paper cycle Mon–Fri at 07:30 London, and a day on which both venues are closed is skipped (#1874, #1957).

## 11. Known gaps (eval 2026-09-25, not fixed here)

- The v2 root reads the local bar store; since #1775 the daily refresh (`server/apps/v2/bar-refresh.ts`) re-pulls it before each real cycle.
- Shadow comparability (§7 deferred item 1): the primary's real fills and the shadows' daily-bar fills are not like-for-like.
- The debate core has two debater seats; the third provider sits out each day by rotation (§4) rather than arguing.
- The CFD short-availability check reads a Saxo reference-data catalogue from a file that the bar refresh rewrites before each non-dry cycle (#1916 part 1); the router itself does not call Saxo, so a failed refresh leaves the last file, which reads `no_catalogue` once it is more than 3 calendar days old (fail closed).
- The 1x gross cap marks at the last bar; a short gapping up inside a session is bounded by the +30% gap sizing, not by the cap.
- Replay harness (§9) limits that need a journal change (#1880 review, 2026-10-01); David ruled on each on 2026-10-01 (doc 66, "replay journal gaps"; built under #1980–#1982):
  - Fixed by #1980: failed calls with no billed usage and `stop_reason` are logged, and the response capture cap is 16,384 characters, above what the pins may return (`JUDGE_MAX_TOKENS` 2048, `DEBATER_MAX_TOKENS` 1024, `server/apps/v2/signal/models.ts`). Every failure row carries the pin's priced id, as an answered call does. An error message that the credential masking or the capture cap changed no longer matches the decision reason, and replays as a `reason` divergence (fail safe).
  - Since #1981 the replay serves US and UK headlines from `v2_news`, so a name whose first prompt hit the 16,384-character prompt cap, or whose news fetch failed, replays from that record. Days before #1981 still read headlines from the first logged prompt, and replay as a `news_error` divergence when it was capped or absent. A name the spend cap refused has its headlines (the fetch runs before the cap check), and since #1987 the replay answers the cap from the day's `llm_spend_cap:` decision rows, so it replays identically. A day whose refusal row is missing replays the name as admitted, which shows as a request the log does not hold.
  - The bars and the CFD catalogue are not copied per day; since #1982 only their digests are journalled (§9). The replay horizon is still bounded: past-day replay works only for days since the last corporate action of a name the day read and the last catalogue refresh. Alpaca bars are pulled with `adjustment: 'all'` (`server/providers/bar-store/alpaca-pull.ts`), so any ex-dividend or split rewrites the history, and the CFD catalogue is rewritten in place and is fresh only when its `asOf` is on or before the trading date (`freshOn`, `server/apps/v2/data/cfd-catalogue.ts`). The limit is now diagnosed rather than silent: such a day reports `input_changed_since` for the name or the catalogue ahead of the `inputs_hash` and decision divergences it causes, so a rewritten input is told apart from a real divergence, but the day still cannot replay identically. `cfdEntryRefusal` reads today's parameters. Litestream backs up only the SQLite store, not the Parquet bars or the catalogue.
- Replay of sizing, orders, fills and marks (#1977) has limits of its own:
  - Since #1983 the rebuilt book repeats every journalled split rescale and anchor (§9). A day journalled before #1983 has no `v2_rescales` rows. The `rescales` stage is compared only once the journal holds a rescale dated on or before the replayed day, so such a day is held to its other rows. A position held across a split on such a day still rebuilds at its pre-split quantity and shows as a `book` divergence (fail safe), and a simulated position's split anchor falls back to the bar before its fill's cycle rather than its fill bar.
  - The half-spread tables are read as they stand when the replay runs. The cycle recomputes the `modelled_slippage_bps` it journals on a broker order from them, so a refresh since the day shows as an `orders` payload divergence. An order journalled before #1884 has no such key, and the replay drops it from the replayed payload before comparing, so a day from before that build replays on its other fields (`comparableTo`, `server/apps/v2/replay-book.ts`).
  - A broker fill journalled before #1983 has no `filled_at`, so the replay serves it at its sweep time: `fillDate()` (`server/apps/v2/cycle.ts`) gives none, and `anchorOpeningFill` and `rescaleHeldToFill` do not run on it as they did live. This shows only when a split falls between the broker fill and its sweep, as a `rescales`, quantity or `marks` divergence (fail safe).
- The first replay run against the real paper store has not been done (#1977): it needs David's Mac or a copy of its store and bar store.
- A day that crashed and was re-run journals both attempts' LLM calls, so it cannot replay identically. The replay reports the extra calls as divergences (fail safe).
- The Alpaca leg's bracket rearm path (`rearmProtectiveLegs`) is not called from the cycle.
- The universe's `MOVERS_MIN_DOLLAR_VOLUME_USD` is a single prior-day dollar volume, not a 20-day average like the liquidity core.
- The 0.6% FX markup on converting USD CFD P&L (doc 69 R13) is not modelled: fills convert at the cycle's rate with no markup and the books hold GBP, so there is no P&L-conversion step to charge it on. At a £15 R it is about £0.09 a trade.
- The CFD short financing credit (`ReceivedCfdInterest`, bid − markdown) is not credited; when the benchmark falls below the markdown a short would pay instead, and that is not modelled either. Saxo's financing page says the borrow fee is fixed when the position opens, while the mark reads each name's current catalogue quote.
- C1 is not measured. Square-root impact coefficients are usually quoted well above 0.05, so simulated impact probably understates cost once capital grows; it is negligible at today's size. A backtest at larger capital must recalibrate it before its costs are trusted.
- A simulated stop fills at the stop price less spread and impact, even when the bar opened through it (gap-through); the real fill would be at or beyond the open. Targets gapped through fill at the target, which is conservative.
- A simulated entry was a limit at the last close before the session. A strategy that buys strength is adversely selected by that order: it fills only on days that trade back down to the prior close, and misses the days that gap away. David ruled 2026-09-27 that the paper entry becomes a marketable limit with a cap (doc 66, [#1815](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/1815)), and on 2026-09-29 set it at 50 bps through the decision close (§7). A limit that far through the close still misses a bar that gaps more than 50 bps away. Session B used the next session's close (doc 70 §2.8).
- A simulated flatten whose name never gets another bar (delisted, suspended) stays pending and keeps the position on the book at its last mark; after 5 calendar days each cycle logs `v2_simulated_flatten_stale`.
- A time-stopped or halted position stays open until the next open (§7, Simulated flattens), so a halt can still lose that open's gap; entries resting at the halting mark are cancelled (§7, Exits).
- After missed cycles, a simulated entry fills on the first bar that reaches its limit, but its bracket is checked only from the bar before the cycle that filled it; bars in between are not checked for the stop or target.
- The simulated entry fill and its same-bar stop exit are two journal writes, not one transaction: a crash between them leaves the position open without the stop exit.
- A split that leaves part of a share floors the held qty to whole shares and books the rest as a `cash_in_lieu` fill (doc 66, "replay journal gaps" ruling 6, #1984). The split, its `v2_rescales` row, the floor and the fill commit in one transaction; a simulated entry's fill commits in the same transaction as its split and floor, opening or add-on. Three gaps remain:
  - Neither venue's cash-in-lieu amount is read, so the fill is priced at the latest close and the book's cash can drift from the broker's until it is paid. The tax log (#1947) flags the disposal; it needs the broker's amount before live (#2001).
  - A position whose split rescale throws (#1927) is rolled back to its pre-split qty and levels. The cycle then blocks every book's entries, skips that position's simulated bracket, ended-series, signal, time-stop, crossing-fill and halt exits and its pending simulated flatten, still exits every other position, and throws before the marks, so the date stays unmarked and the next cycle retries the rescale. In production that retry is the next weekday's scheduled run (`ops/launchd/com.samurai.v2-paper.plist`, weekdays 07:30), which runs under its own date; the unmarked date itself is marked only if a person re-runs it with `--date`. Broker legs keep their venue-resting stops. Nothing bounds a deterministic throw (a constraint failure, a corrupt split input): it recurs every cycle, so the date stays unmarked, the position gets no simulated exit, and a simulated book's position has no stop at all until the cause is fixed. Each cycle logs `v2_split_rescale_threw` and `v2_split_rescale_unmarked` at error level, which the v2 alerting logger sends as critical alerts. Nothing escalates after repeated cycles.
  - A broker stop left stale by a split is cancelled and re-placed (David 2026-10-02, doc 66, #1990). The venues' behaviour comes from search-result extracts, since neither venue's pages could be fetched from the build environment. For Alpaca's corporate-actions and order pages, they say Alpaca adjusts the price and qty of a GTC sell stop on a forward split, cancels GTC orders on a reverse split, and adjusts or cancels neither leg of a bracket, which it sends do-not-reduce/do-not-cancel. For Saxo's help centre, they say Saxo deletes open orders the day before a split's ex-date. Alpaca cannot change a bracket leg's qty, only its stop or limit price. Reconcile compares each held position's closing-side stops with the ledger: the stops' remaining qty (qty less filled qty) must sum to the position, and each stop price must sit within two ticks of the held entry's journalled stop divided by the position's split factor. A difference is a `protective_qty` or `protective_price` diff, so the venue's reconcile is a mismatch, `v2_reconcile_mismatch` goes out as a critical alert, and the venue's books block entries until a reconcile is clean. The check skips a position whose entry, or an add-on entry on the same name, is still open at the venue, since that bracket's stop is sized for the whole order. A bracket whose part-filled entry was cancelled is checked; if Alpaca leaves its legs at the whole order's qty (unverified), that shows as `protective_qty` and is replaced like a split. A held position with no closing stop at the venue (`position_unprotected`) whose held entry has a journalled stop, and whose entry is no longer open at the venue, is re-armed the same way (David 2026-10-02, doc 66 ruling 4): a crash between cancel and place, a cancel that completes after the cycle gave up on it, a reverse split that cancels GTC orders, or a refused flatten all leave this state. One without a journalled stop keeps the mismatch alert and the entry block alone. For each broker-routed position on such a name, the cycle then sends a risk-approved `replace_stop` order (`v2-<book>-<date>-<instrument>-restop`, journalled as an exit leg with the step that failed, if one did). The Alpaca adapter cancels the position's resting target and stop legs, `held` ones included, and any resting re-arm OCO, and confirms each cancelled; with nothing resting it cancels nothing. Only then does it read the broker position again and place an OCO at the ledger stop and target on the rearm wire ids, sized to the lesser of the ledger qty and the broker's on the position's side, so a stop that filled or oversold during the cancel cannot leave an oversized or exposure-adding OCO; with nothing left held on that side it places nothing, and the cycle logs that nothing was placed. The venue never holds two closing stops. When the cancel confirms, the window with no stop is one round-trip; when it times out, the window lasts until the next run, which a same-date re-run closes. A refused place flattens the position at market (`stop_replace_failed`) and logs `v2_stop_replace_failed`; a cancel that does not confirm sends nothing more and logs `v2_stop_cancel_failed`; a replace refused before the adapter sent any cancel logs `v2_stop_replace_refused` with its reason; a position with a stale stop and no journalled entry stop is not touched and logs `v2_stop_replace_unpriced`. All four are error level, sent as critical alerts. A same-date re-run whose reconcile still finds the stop stale or missing sends the replace again under the next journal id (`-restop-2`, then `-3` and `-4`): a cancel that timed out and completed late, or a refused flatten, is re-armed within that run. After four a date the cycle logs `v2_stop_replace_exhausted` at error level and waits for the next date. A clean reconcile sends nothing, and a crash before the order is journalled re-runs the same id; either way the adapter cancels and confirms any OCO an earlier run placed before it places on the next rearm wire id, so a re-run never leaves two. Each attempt that places spends one of the entry's four rearm wire ids, and an entry that has spent them fails the place, which flattens. A replay mirrors a journalled reconcile whose only differences were stale or missing stops with those stops (a missing one left out), and re-serves each journalled attempt's outcome in suffix order. A stray, or a position with an exit pending, is not replaced. Every broker-held rescale also logs `v2_split_broker_check` at error level. A forward split's stale stop sits above the new price and can fire at the ex-date open, before the cycle has seen the split; nothing acts before the ex-date.
