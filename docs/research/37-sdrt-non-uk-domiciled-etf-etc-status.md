# 37 — Does 0.5% SDRT apply to LSE-listed, non-UK-domiciled ETFs/ETCs?

**Researched 2026-08-26** for [#908](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/908), under map [#631](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/631). Closes the specific unresolved item [`35-broker-venue-algo-trading-terms.md`](35-broker-venue-algo-trading-terms.md) flagged and could not close from broker documents: *"ADR-0015 asserts no SDRT on GBP LSE ETFs/ETCs, and brokers' own pages say only '0.5% on purchases of UK shares' without addressing ETFs. No primary source confirming the ETF exemption was reached in this pass."* This doc goes to the statute (Finance Act 1986), the implementing regulations, and HMRC's own manual and guidance — not a broker's marketing copy — the same discipline [`36-isa-qualifying-etc-etn-status.md`](36-isa-qualifying-etc-etn-status.md) applied to the ISA-eligibility question this doc's sibling closed.

## TL;DR — verdict

**No SDRT on ADR-0016's universe, and ADR-0015's assumption is confirmed correct — but the two sub-classes clear the charge by different, non-symmetric routes, and the ETC/ETN route rests on one checkable issuer fact rather than a blanket statutory carve-out.**

- **The controlling test is the security issuer's place of incorporation, not the venue where the security trades — confirmed directly from the statute.** Finance Act 1986 s.99(4): *"'Chargeable securities' does not include securities falling within paragraph (a), (b) or (c) of subsection (3) above which are issued or raised by a body corporate not incorporated in the United Kingdom unless — (a) they are registered in a register kept in the United Kingdom by or on behalf of the body corporate by which they are issued or raised, or (b) in the case of shares, they are paired with shares issued by a body corporate incorporated in the United Kingdom, or (c) in the case of securities falling within paragraph (b) or (c) of subsection (3) above, paragraph (a) or (b) above applies to the stocks, shares or loan capital to which they relate, or (d) they are issued or raised by a UK Societas…"* — fetched verbatim from legislation.gov.uk. **Nothing in s.99 or in FA1986 s.87 (the charging section) mentions the exchange a security trades on.** HMRC's own plain-English guidance states the same test in one sentence: *"Stamp Duty Reserve Tax is incurred on electronic (or 'paperless') transactions in shares in a UK company or a foreign company with a UK share register"* (`gov.uk/government/publications/stamp-duty-and-stamp-duty-reserve-tax`).
- **An LSE listing does not re-import the charge.** Section 99(4)'s only four re-entry routes are: (a) a UK-kept register, (b) pairing with UK-incorporated shares, (c) the same two conditions flowing through an "interest in" or "right to" the security, or (d) a UK Societas election. **"Admitted to trading on the London Stock Exchange" is not one of the four**, and no other provision in Part IV of FA1986 makes trading venue a chargeability trigger. Getting onto the LSE is what satisfies a *different* test (the "recognised stock exchange" listing condition that ISA-eligibility and the UCITS-ETF exemption both use — see doc 36 and below) — it plays no role in the SDRT chargeable-securities gate.
- **ETFs and ETCs/ETNs are exempt by two different, non-overlapping legal routes, and only one of the two is unconditional on incorporation.**
  - **UCITS ETFs (Irish/Luxembourg-domiciled) clear on two independent bases.** First, the general s.99(4) non-UK-incorporation exclusion applies to them exactly as it does to any other foreign company's shares — an Irish ICAV or Luxembourg SICAV is a company issuing shares, not UK-incorporated. Second, and **unconditionally, regardless of incorporation or register location**, the Stamp Duty and Stamp Duty Reserve Tax (Exchange Traded Funds) (Exemption) Regulations 2014 (SI 2014/911) reg 3 provide: *"Stamp duty reserve tax shall not be chargeable on a transfer or an agreement to transfer units in an exchange traded fund."* SI 2014/911 reg 5 defines "exchange traded fund" as a collective investment scheme that is an open-ended investment company admitted to trading on a regulated market or MTF **and** *"authorised as a UCITS…pursuant to Directive 2009/65/EC"* — and HMRC's own manual (STSM101060) confirms this reaches **both** UK and EEA funds: *"Section 236A of FSMA defines (inter alia) a UCITS as being an undertaking established in the United Kingdom or an EEA State and so covers UK and EEA ETFs."* Ireland and Luxembourg are EEA states. This exemption attaches to the *transaction* ("a transfer… of units"), not to a redefinition of "chargeable securities" — so it reaches the units regardless of how they settle (direct CREST, International CREST, or a CDI wrapper), closing the depositary-interest question below for this sub-class specifically.
  - **ETCs/ETNs (debt-structured) clear on one basis only — s.99(4) — and it is conditional on where the issuer keeps its register, not on the security being debt.** This is the answer to the task's framing question 3, and it cuts against the intuition that a debt-security exemption (the "loan capital" relief) is what saves ETCs/ETNs. **It is not.** FA1986 s.79 (the plain-vanilla loan-capital exemption STSM cites as *"loan capital (FA86/S79), are exempt from SDRT"*) is a UK-company-specific relief with its own conditions (no conversion rights, no interest tied to profits or exceeding a reasonable commercial return) that a leverage- or index-linked note would not obviously satisfy, and it never needs to be reached here: s.99(4) already defines securities issued by a non-UK-incorporated body corporate as **outside** the "chargeable securities" universe before the loan-capital question would even arise, and s.99(4)'s carve-out expressly covers *"stocks, shares or loan capital"* alike — it draws no distinction between the two. So an ETC/ETN issued by a Jersey or Irish SPV is exempt for the same structural reason a UCITS ETF's shares are (non-UK incorporation), not because it happens to be debt.
- **The ETC/ETN sub-class's exemption therefore turns on one verifiable fact per issuer: is its securities register kept in the UK?** Checked directly against one major ADR-0016-class issuer's own base prospectus (WisdomTree Issuer X Limited, a Jersey plc — the same document doc 36 read for the ISA question): *"for so long as the Uncertificated Registered Securities are participating securities: (i) **the Register shall be maintained in Jersey and at all times outside of the United Kingdom**…"* — a contractual condition of the Trust Deed, not an incidental fact, confirming the structure is deliberately built to keep the register offshore. Leverage Shares plc (Ireland-incorporated, confirmed from its own FCA-approved base prospectus) is likewise non-UK-incorporated, consistent with the same fact pattern, though its register location was not independently confirmed to the same clause-level detail (see Confidence, below). **On the evidence gathered, the condition is satisfied**, but this is a fact about each issuer's trust deed, not a blanket statutory guarantee — a hypothetical issuer that kept a UK register would lose the exemption despite being non-UK-incorporated and LSE-listed.
- **Quantified at ADR-0018 D5's ticket sizes, if SDRT did NOT apply (the confirmed position): £0.** If it did apply (the counterfactual, relevant only if a specific line failed the register check): **+£1.75 on a £350 index buy, +£1.25 on a £250 single-stock buy — +0.50 percentage points added to round-trip cost, purchase-side only** (SDRT has no sell-side leg). Against doc 52's measured per-trade geometry bars (~1.3–7.2 pp) that is **~39% of PLTR's 1.29 pp bar** and **~7% of MSTR's 7.16 pp bar**; against doc 54's break-even-accuracy framing it would add roughly **+0.5 pp to the required win rate**, on top of the LLM-bill terms doc 54 already carries. Because the tax attaches to the **instrument**, not the venue, it would (if it applied) hit every broker in doc 35's survey identically and would **not** reorder that venue ranking — it would shift the whole cost floor uniformly, exactly as the task framing anticipated.

**Bottom line: not conditional in practice, but conditional in structure.** The verdict is "no SDRT" for the whole ADR-0016 universe on the evidence gathered — the ETF sub-class unconditionally (SI 2014/911), the ETC/ETN sub-class because the checkable register fact comes back negative (no UK register) for the one issuer examined in clause-level detail, consistent with the general fact pattern (non-UK incorporation) for a second issuer. It is not "no SDRT, full stop, as a matter of what an ETC is" — it is "no SDRT, as a matter of how these specific issuers structured their trust deeds," which is a narrower and more falsifiable claim than ADR-0015's flat assertion, and one a new ADR-0016 issuer should be checked against before being added to the pool.

## 1. Does SDRT depend on the issuer's place of incorporation, or the venue of trade?

**Incorporation (and, conditionally, register location) — not venue. Confirmed directly from FA1986 s.87 and s.99.**

Section 87(1) creates the charge on *"an agreement… to transfer chargeable securities… for consideration in money or money's worth"*; s.87(6) sets the rate at 0.5% of the consideration. Nowhere in s.87 does the charge test where the agreement is made, where the securities are listed, or through what exchange the transfer is executed — it tests only whether the thing being transferred is a "chargeable security" and whether it is transferred for consideration. That is entirely s.99's question.

Section 99(3) defines the base class: *"'chargeable securities' means — (a) stocks, shares or loan capital, (b) interests in, or in dividends or other rights arising out of, stocks, shares or loan capital, (c) rights to allotments of or to subscribe for, or options to acquire, stocks, shares or loan capital, and (d) units under a unit trust scheme."* Section 99(4), quoted in full above, then removes from that class *"securities falling within paragraph (a), (b) or (c) of subsection (3)… which are issued or raised by a body corporate not incorporated in the United Kingdom"* unless one of four re-entry conditions is met. **The operative fact the statute tests is where the issuing body corporate is incorporated.** HMRC's manual restates this as a company-status test (STSM031090), and HMRC's own plain-English guidance collapses it to a single sentence any retail investor can read: *"Stamp Duty Reserve Tax is incurred on electronic… transactions in shares in a UK company or a foreign company with a UK share register."* Both halves of that sentence are the same test s.99(4) states in statute — "UK company" (incorporation) or "foreign company with a UK share register" (the s.99(4)(a) re-entry route).

This directly answers the crux doc 35 could not resolve: **an Irish/Luxembourg-incorporated UCITS ETF issuer and a Jersey/Irish-incorporated ETC/ETN SPV are both "a body corporate not incorporated in the United Kingdom,"** and their securities start outside the chargeable-securities definition by default, regardless of the fact that the securities in question trade on the LSE.

## 2. Does an LSE listing re-import the SDRT charge?

**No — LSE admission is not one of s.99(4)'s four re-entry conditions, and no other FA1986 provision makes trading venue a chargeability trigger.**

The only routes back into charge for a non-UK-incorporated issuer's securities are: **(a)** a UK-kept register, **(b)** shares paired with UK-incorporated shares, **(c)** the same two conditions applying through an "interest in" or "right to" the underlying security (i.e., you cannot escape (a)/(b) one derivative layer removed), or **(d)** a UK Societas election (an EU-company-law mechanism not applicable to any ADR-0016 issuer type). Trading on a UK exchange is conspicuously absent from that list, and the general SDRT literature (Clearstream's and Euronext's own investor-facing SDRT overviews, cited above only as corroborating professional-market summaries, not as primary authority) describe the same four-limb test with the same absence.

This is not a coincidence of drafting — the "recognised stock exchange" / "admitted to trading" test that *does* matter elsewhere in this analysis is a **different** gate, used for a different purpose:
- It is what SI 2014/911 reg 5 tests when it requires an ETF to be *"admitted to trading on… a regulated market, or… a multilateral trading facility"* before the ETF exemption attaches (a structural precondition of that specific relief, unrelated to s.99(4)).
- It is what ISA Regulations 1998 reg 7(5) tests for a security to be ISA-qualifying at all — the subject of doc 36, a wholly separate statutory scheme (the ISA Regulations) from FA1986's SDRT charge.

Both of those are genuine listing/admission-to-trading tests, and an LSE listing satisfies both. **Neither of them is the SDRT chargeability test**, and satisfying them has no bearing on s.99(4). An LSE-listed, non-UK-incorporated security clears the SDRT gate by staying off a UK register — not by being listed anywhere in particular.

## 3. Are ETCs/ETNs treated differently from ETFs? — checking the loan-capital exclusion specifically

**Not via the loan-capital route the task hypothesised — via a different exemption altogether, and this is the most important correction this research makes to the naïve framing.**

FA1986 s.99(5) (restated at STSM041020) provides that *"'chargeable securities' will not include securities the transfer of which is exempt from all stamp duty,"* and separately, s.79 FA1986 grants stamp duty (and, through s.99(5), SDRT) exemption to "plain vanilla" loan capital. This is the mechanism the task's framing anticipated might carve out ETC/ETN structures as debt securities. **Section 79's own text was not fetched in this pass**, so no view is taken here on whether a leverage- or index-linked note would satisfy its conditions — that question is unresolved and, on current facts, does not need resolving.

**It is unnecessary here because it is not reached at all.** **The question never arises for a non-UK-incorporated issuer**, because s.99(4) removes the security from the "chargeable securities" universe *before* s.99(5)/s.79's loan-capital test would be reached — s.99(4) is a **prior, independent** gate that applies to *"stocks, shares or loan capital"* without distinction (the statutory text names all three in the same breath: *"securities falling within paragraph (a), (b) or (c) of subsection (3)"*, and s.99(3)(a) itself reads *"stocks, shares or loan capital"* as a single undifferentiated category). **The exclusion doesn't care that an ETC/ETN is debt; it cares that the issuer is not a UK company.** If a future ADR-0016 issuer ever failed the register check in §5 below, s.79's loan-capital relief is the fallback that would then need checking on its own terms — and it has not been confirmed to apply, so that fallback should not be assumed available.

This means ETFs and ETCs/ETNs are **not** treated differently by a debt-vs-equity distinction in the way the framing suggested. They are treated differently by a **different** distinction — fund-wrapper vs. plain-corporate-issuer, exactly the axis doc 36 identified for the ISA question:

| Sub-class | Primary exemption route | Conditional on incorporation/register? | Conditional on settlement mechanism? |
|---|---|---|---|
| UCITS ETF (Irish/Luxembourg) | SI 2014/911 reg 3 — exempts the **transaction** in ETF units, unconditionally for any UK/EEA UCITS admitted to a regulated market/MTF | **No** — applies regardless of issuer incorporation | **No** — a transaction-level exemption reaches the units however held/settled (see §4) |
| UCITS ETF (Irish/Luxembourg) | s.99(4) — non-UK-incorporation exclusion (secondary/backup route, same as any other foreign company's shares) | **Yes**, but satisfied by construction (Irish/Luxembourg incorporation) | N/A at this route (chargeable-securities status, not a transaction exemption) |
| ETC/ETN (Jersey/Irish SPV) | s.99(4) only — no ETC/ETN-specific or debt-specific exemption exists or is needed | **Yes** — turns on the issuer's register location, checked in §5 | Not reached if s.99(4) already excludes the security |

**The practical result is identical (no SDRT) for both sub-classes on the evidence gathered, but the ETF sub-class has an unconditional backstop the ETC/ETN sub-class does not.** If a future ADR-0016 candidate issuer ever kept a UK register for its ETC/ETN line, the ETF-style unconditional exemption would not be available to rescue it — there is no SI-2014/911 equivalent for debt-structured ETPs.

## 4. Does a CREST Depositary Interest (CDI) wrapper change any of this?

**Checked because it's the obvious mechanical objection, and it doesn't change the verdict for either sub-class, for two different reasons.**

Some foreign securities historically reached CREST settlement only via a UK-issued CDI — a separate instrument (itself potentially a "chargeable security" under s.99(3)(b) as an *"interest in… stocks, shares or loan capital"*) representing the foreign security. If ADR-0016 lines settled this way, the CDI itself would need its own exemption. The Stamp Duty Reserve Tax (UK Depositary Interests in Foreign Securities) Regulations 1999 (SI 1999/2383) reg 3(1) provide exactly that: *"a UK depositary interest in foreign securities is not a chargeable security…"*, conditional (reg 2) on the underlying issuer being non-UK-incorporated and non-UK-centrally-managed, not on a UK register, and — the wrinkle — **not representing units/shares in a collective investment scheme.**

That CIS carve-out would, on its own, leave a CDI-wrapped UCITS ETF outside SI 1999/2383's relief. **It does not matter, because SI 2014/911 reg 3 exempts the underlying transaction — "a transfer… of units in an exchange traded fund" — directly, not by redefining what counts as a chargeable security.** A transaction-level exemption reaches the ETF units regardless of the settlement mechanism sitting underneath them, so the CDI/CIS wrinkle is closed for the ETF sub-class without needing to resolve whether any ADR-0016 UCITS ETF actually settles via a CDI at all.

For the ETC/ETN sub-class, the question is largely moot on the evidence gathered because the issuer examined does not use a CDI wrapper for UK settlement in the first place: WisdomTree Issuer X Limited's Conditions state *"the initial Relevant Clearing System will be CREST"* and describe the securities as **directly** issued in *"dematerialised uncertificated registered form"*, cleared through CREST as *"participating securities"* — i.e., the WT Securities themselves are the CREST-eligible instrument, not a CDI standing in for them. Direct CREST-eligibility for a non-UK-incorporated issuer's securities is unremarkable — CREST (operated by Euroclear UK & Ireland) has supported non-UK issuers' securities directly since well before 2001 specifically so that a CDI wrapper is not always needed. Since there is no CDI in this structure, SI 1999/2383 is not the operative relief at all; s.99(4) applies directly to the WT Securities themselves, and the question collapses to the register-location fact in §5.

## 5. The one checkable fact: where is the ETC/ETN issuer's register kept?

**Confirmed, for the issuer checked in clause-level detail: outside the United Kingdom, by explicit contractual condition.**

WisdomTree Issuer X Limited's Conditions (base prospectus, Condition 2, "Form and Title"): *"Notwithstanding anything to the contrary in the Conditions, for so long as the Uncertificated Registered Securities are participating securities: (i) **the Register shall be maintained in Jersey and at all times outside of the United Kingdom**, (ii) the Uncertificated Registered Securities may be issued in uncertificated form in accordance with and subject as provided in the Uncertificated Regulations… "* — where "the Uncertificated Regulations" are defined as *"the Companies (Uncertificated Securities) (Jersey) Order 1999."* This is not an incidental fact buried in boilerplate; it is a deliberately drafted condition of the Trust Deed governing every WT Security issued under the programme, consistent with an issuer that has structured itself to keep s.99(4)(a)'s re-entry route closed.

Leverage Shares Public Limited Company is independently confirmed, from its own FCA-approved base prospectus, to be *"a public limited company incorporated in Ireland."* Its prospectus has **no** SDRT/"chargeable securities" section at all to quote a register clause from — a UK-facing base prospectus's silence on a cost that would apply to every UK retail purchase is itself mildly informative (a live UK-register SDRT liability is exactly the kind of investor cost a prospectus would need to flag), but this is inference from absence, not a quoted fact, and is flagged as such in Confidence below.

**This is the one place the task's framing and this doc's finding diverge from a clean "the statute settles it" answer.** The statute and HMRC's guidance settle the *test*; whether any given ADR-0016 issuer's trust deed satisfies that test is a fact about that issuer's own documents, checkable in the same way doc 36 checked WisdomTree's "company" status against its own prospectus rather than assuming it from the entity's name. On the evidence gathered here, the check comes back negative (no UK register) for the issuer examined in full detail, and the fact pattern (non-UK incorporation, no disclosed UK-register cost) is consistent for the second issuer checked at the incorporation level alone.

## 6. Quantified effect at D5's £350 / £250 ticket sizes

**Confirmed position (no SDRT applies): £0, exactly as ADR-0015 assumed and as doc 35's cost table already priced in.**

**Counterfactual A — a single ADR-0016 line failed the register check** (a per-line edge case; not the current finding for any issuer checked):

| | Index ticket (£350) | Single-stock ticket (£250) |
|---|---|---|
| SDRT (0.5% of purchase consideration, **entry only** — no SDRT on the sale leg) | £1.75 | £1.25 |
| As added position-notional cost (0.5% of the £350/£250 purchase consideration, stated the way doc 35/52 state their cost figures — as a % of position notional, entry-side only, not summed against a round-trip denominator) | **+0.50 pp** | **+0.50 pp** |

| Against doc 54's break-even-accuracy framing (which states costs as accuracy points, e.g. the £58/yr LLM bill ≈ 0.55–1.58 pp) | roughly **+0.5 pp** added to the required win rate, on top of the LLM-bill terms doc 54 already carries | roughly **+0.5 pp** added |

**Counterfactual B — the whole ETC/ETN sub-class turned out to be chargeable** (the universe-wide version the task's framing actually asked for, since s.99(4)'s non-UK-incorporation test is common to every ETC/ETN issuer in the pool, not issuer-specific in its legal basis — only the register-location *fact* varies by issuer). If the register check failed as a general matter rather than for one line, every ETC/ETN entry in the universe — not just one ticker — would carry the same +£1.75/+£1.25 (+0.50 pp) shown above, on every purchase, indefinitely. That is not a rounding adjustment to ADR-0015's cost table; ADR-0015 lists "No SDRT, no FX" as part of the *reason* GBP LSE ETFs/ETCs are "the only viable class," so this counterfactual would require rewriting that table's middle row, not annotating it, and would move doc 54's break-even accuracy bar for the entire ETC/ETN half of the universe simultaneously. UCITS ETF lines would be unaffected by this counterfactual (SI 2014/911's exemption does not depend on the register-location fact at all).

**Because the charge (in either counterfactual) would attach to the instrument rather than the venue, it would hit every broker in doc 35's survey identically** — T212, Saxo, IBKR, IG and every other candidate would all see the same +0.50 pp on the same ADR-0016 lines. It would shift doc 35's entire cost floor uniformly rather than reordering the venue ranking (Saxo would still be ~8bps cheaper per side than IBKR's £3 floor either way) — exactly the "universe-wide, not venue-specific" shape the task's framing anticipated, now confirmed rather than assumed.

## 7. A forward-looking note — the 2027 Securities Transfer Tax reform

**Secondary source, not load-bearing for the current-law verdict, recorded because it independently corroborates the incorporation-based test's direction of travel.** HM Treasury published draft legislation (13 July 2026) for a Securities Transfer Tax (STT) intended to replace stamp duty/SDRT from 2027, described by Mayer Brown's summary as retaining the existing 0.5%/1.5% rate structure while *simplifying* the non-UK-issuer test: *"direct transfers of ordinary shares in non-UK-incorporated companies should generally fall outside STT, even if the company is UK tax resident… the current incorporation-based test should apply consistently, without needing to analyze where the share register is maintained."* If accurate, the reform would (for shares, at least) remove the register-location wrinkle this doc's §5 turns on, converting the ETC/ETN sub-class's conditional exemption into something closer to the ETF sub-class's unconditional one. This is not verified against the draft legislation itself in this pass and should not be treated as settled — it is a reason to re-check this doc against the STT's final form before 2027, not a reason to discount the current-law analysis above.

## Scope — what this doc does and does not settle

This is a **statute-and-manual-level, issuer-structural** finding. It does not, and cannot, settle:

1. **Whether every ADR-0016 candidate ISIN's specific issuer keeps its register outside the UK.** Confirmed in clause-level detail for one issuer (WisdomTree Issuer X Limited) and at the incorporation level only for a second (Leverage Shares plc). Boost/WisdomTree Boost and GraniteShares prospectuses were not pulled in this pass. A five-minute prospectus check per remaining issuer (the same discipline doc 36 recommends for its own residual ambiguity) would close this completely.
2. **Whether a specific broker's execution/settlement pathway inserts a UK-registered intermediary step that could, in principle, alter the analysis for that broker's clients specifically.** Nothing in doc 35's broker survey suggested this, but it was not the question either doc asked.
3. **The SI 2014/911 "regulated market or MTF" condition for every ADR-0016 UCITS ETF line individually** — the LSE Main Market is unambiguously a UK regulated market, so this is not in doubt for any LSE-listed line, but it was not checked ticker-by-ticker.
4. **The 1.5% higher-rate SDRT charge** (transfers into a clearance service or depositary receipt system, FA1986 s.93/s.96, referenced only in passing above) — not analysed here, because ordinary retail purchases through a broker are not transfers *into* a clearance/depositary system in the sense that charge targets; this doc addresses only the 0.5% principal charge on an ordinary purchase, which is what the task and ADR-0015 both ask about.

## Confidence and residual ambiguity

**High confidence on the statutory test itself.** FA1986 s.87's charging language, s.99(3)'s base definition, and s.99(4)'s non-UK-incorporation exclusion (all four re-entry limbs) were read from the current in-force text of the Act via legislation.gov.uk's structured data endpoint, specifically to avoid a summarizing intermediary's paraphrase — an earlier plain-page fetch of the same section returned editorialised prose and was discarded in favour of the XML-sourced text quoted above. That text was independently cross-checked against HMRC's own STSM manual restating the identical test, and against HMRC's plain-English public guidance stating the same test in one sentence. Three independent primary sources (statute, internal manual, public guidance) converge on the same rule with no daylight between them.

**High confidence on the ETF sub-class's unconditional exemption.** SI 2014/911's regs 3 and 5 were fetched and quoted verbatim; the UK/EEA scope of "UCITS" for this purpose is stated explicitly in HMRC's own manual (STSM101060), not inferred.

**Good, but not exhaustive, confidence on the ETC/ETN sub-class's conditional exemption.** The mechanism (s.99(4) alone, turning on register location) is established with statute-level confidence. The **fact** that ADR-0016-class issuers actually satisfy it was confirmed at clause level for one issuer and at incorporation level only for a second; it was not checked for every candidate issuer, and no primary source in this pass states "this specific ISIN is SDRT-exempt" the way an HMRC ruling or a UK-specific prospectus tax opinion would. The genuinely UK-facing prospectus for at least one issuer's LSE-listed range (WisdomTree's own referenced "UK Prospectus") was not located or read — the register clause quoted here comes from the general/EU programme document, which is strong circumstantial evidence of programme-wide structuring but not a UK-tax-specific representation.

**Residual ambiguity, narrow and operational rather than legal:**

- Whether Boost/WisdomTree Boost and GraniteShares (the other major ADR-0016-class issuers named in doc 36) keep their registers outside the UK was not checked; nothing found here suggests a different fact pattern, given all trade under non-UK "plc"/"Limited" issuer names via the same LSE ETC/ETN admission route doc 36 documented.
- Whether any ADR-0016 line settles via a CDI wrapper rather than directly in CREST was not confirmed either way for issuers other than WisdomTree Issuer X (confirmed direct, no CDI). If a CDI wrapper is used and represents an ETC/ETN (not a fund, so SI 2014/911 would not rescue it), SI 1999/2383's exemption would need its own four conditions checked — not a live concern for the issuer checked, but worth flagging for any future issuer that does use CDIs.
- The 2027 Securities Transfer Tax reform (§7) is drawn from a secondary summary of draft legislation, not the draft legislation itself, and is recorded only as a forward-looking corroboration, not a finding this doc stands behind to statute-level confidence.
