# 36 — Are LSE leveraged ETC/ETN-structured ETPs ISA-qualifying investments, venue-independently?

**Researched 2026-08-26** for [#907](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/907), under map #631. Answers the regulation-first version of the question [#665](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/665) asked narrowly about Trading 212, and closes the "shared, unresolved risk" doc 35 flagged and could not close from broker documents alone: *"no firm's documents establish that ADR-0016's LSE leveraged ETPs — commonly ETC/ETN-structured — are ISA-qualifying investments at their venue."* This doc goes to the source those documents were silent about: HM Treasury's own Regulations and HMRC's own guidance, not a broker's marketing or account-opening copy.

## TL;DR — verdict

**Qualifying, venue-independently, and leverage is irrelevant to the answer.** LSE-listed ETC/ETN-structured products are debt securities issued by a company, and the Individual Savings Account Regulations 1998 (SI 1998/1870) qualify debt securities under a **structural** test — issuer type plus stock-exchange listing — that says nothing about what the debt security's payout formula tracks or how leveraged it is. Specifically:

- **The legal hook is regulation 7(2)(b): "qualifying securities."** A security is qualifying if (i) it is *"issued by a company wherever incorporated"* and (ii) it satisfies one of regulation 7(5)'s listing conditions. For an FCA-listed LSE Main Market line the operative condition is **reg 7(5)(b) — "that the securities are so listed"** (i.e. officially listed) — since the LSE's own admission route for ETCs/ETNs is to *"apply to the FCA for a London listing and to London Stock Exchange for admission to trading"*, both conditions are typically met at once; reg 7(5)(e) — *"that the securities are so admitted to trading"* — is the fallback that would independently qualify a line admitted to trading but not on the official list. ETC and ETN products are, by construction, debt instruments (*"loan stock or similar security… whether secured or unsecured"*, in HMRC's own words) issued by a special-purpose-vehicle company and admitted to trading on the LSE Main Market. **They clear reg 7(2)(b)/(5) on the same test a corporate bond or a Eurobond clears it** — nothing in the regulation, and nothing in HMRC's guidance, singles out ETCs or ETNs for different treatment, either favourable or unfavourable.
- **Embedded leverage and embedded derivatives do not appear anywhere in the qualifying-investment test.** Reg 7 tests *who issued the security and where it trades*, never *what economic exposure it delivers*. A 3x daily-leveraged note and a 1x note from the same issuer, admitted on the same exchange, are identical under reg 7 — the regulation has no leverage clause, no derivative-content clause, no "plain vanilla" requirement to fail.
- **The one place the Regulations *do* single out an ETN by asset class is cryptoassets, and that carve-out is the strongest evidence for the general rule, not an exception to it.** Regulation 7(2G)/(2H), added for 6 April 2026, excludes "a UK cryptoasset exchange traded note" specifically from being a qualifying security under 2(b) (with pre-existing holdings grandfathered). HMRC's own policy paper on this change states crypto ETNs were, immediately before that, *"automatically eligible for inclusion in stocks and shares ISAs"* once the FCA lifted its retail-distribution ban in October 2025 — with **no ISA-regulation amendment needed to make them eligible in the first place.** That is only possible if the general "qualifying securities" test already reached ETN-structured products before anyone touched crypto. Parliament then had to write a bespoke exclusion to carve *crypto* ETNs back out. No equivalent exclusion exists, or has ever existed, for equity-index or commodity ETNs/ETCs — leveraged or not.
- **UCITS ETFs qualify by a different, fund-regulation hook (reg 7(2)(ga)): "shares… of a UK UCITS, a recognised UCITS or a non-UCITS retail scheme… listed on or admitted to trading."** That route requires the *issuer* to be an FCA-authorised or FCA-recognised collective investment scheme in the legal form of an Authorised Unit Trust, ICVC or Authorised Contractual Scheme (confirmed by the London Stock Exchange's own ETF/ETC/ETN admission factsheet, which routes ETFs and ETCs/ETNs through **different admission processes**). An ETC/ETN issuer is deliberately **not** a fund and carries none of that authorisation — it is a plain corporate entity issuing debt, which is why it needs the *securities* hook (2(b)) rather than the *fund-units* hook (2(ga)). This is the real answer to "what makes them different in the regulation's eyes": **wrapper structure (fund vs. debt), not eligibility tier.** Both hooks lead to the same qualifying outcome; UCITS ETFs and ETC/ETN notes are simply routed there by different regulation 7 sub-paragraphs, each with its own conditions, and a UCITS wrapper additionally cannot legally carry the leverage ADR-0016's instruments carry (UCITS leverage/diversification limits), which is *why* ADR-0016's universe had to be ETC/ETN-structured at all.
- **The listing condition is not in doubt for LSE lines and is not part of what's ambiguous here.** The London Stock Exchange's own ETC/ETN admission factsheet states the admission route directly: *"ETCs & ETNs can be admitted to trading on the London Stock Exchange via the route below: Apply to the FCA for a London listing and to London Stock Exchange for admission to trading on the Main Market."* Reaching the LSE Main Market at all **is** satisfying an FCA listing / admission-to-trading process — the same process reg 7(5) tests. A line that is on the LSE has, by definition of having gotten there, cleared the condition reg 7(5) asks about. There is no additional, LSE-specific listing gate hiding behind this that a leveraged ETP could fail while a plain one passes.

**What is NOT resolved by this doc, and is not a regulatory question:** whether a *specific broker's* systems correctly classify a *specific* ADR-0016 ISIN as ISA-eligible in its own instrument master data, and whether that broker's FCA-mandated retail-distribution appropriateness check for complex products (a COBS/PRIIPs conduct-of-business gate, not an ISA-eligibility gate — this is what T212's "complex products questionnaire" in #665 tests) lets a retail client actually buy the line inside the ISA. Those are operational/venue questions, already the subject of doc 35 and #665, and this doc's finding narrows but does not eliminate them: the law reaches these products, so a venue refusing to hold one is a venue policy or data gap, not a legal bar the venue is correctly enforcing.

## The regulation's anatomy — three separate qualifying hooks, one listing test

Regulation 7(1) of SI 1998/1870 defines *"qualifying investments for a stocks and shares component"* and then lists them in paragraph (2). Three sub-paragraphs matter here, and they are separately gated:

| Reg 7(2) sub-para | What it admits | Issuer/vehicle test | Listing test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a)** — shares | Equity, *"issued by a company wherever incorporated"* | Any company (as defined in reg 2) | Officially listed on a recognised stock exchange, **or** admitted to trading on a recognised stock exchange in the UK/EEA |
| **(b)** — "qualifying securities" | **Debt** — *"issued by a company wherever incorporated"*, satisfying reg 7(5) | Any company (as defined in reg 2) | Reg 7(5)(a)–(f): the security itself, or the issuer's shares, or a 75%-subsidiary chain, either officially listed **or** admitted to trading, UK/EEA |
| **(ga)** — UCITS/NURS fund units | *"shares of a UK UCITS, a recognised UCITS or a non-UCITS retail scheme"* | Must be an **FCA-authorised or FCA-recognised collective investment scheme** — fund regulation, not company law | Listed on or admitted to trading on a recognised stock exchange |

Regulation 2 defines *"company"*, for reg 7 purposes, as *"any body corporate having a share capital other than — (i) an open-ended investment company within the meaning given by section 236 of [FSMA 2000], or (ii) a UK UCITS, recognised UCITS or non-UCITS retail scheme."* That is a **negative** definition: everything with share capital qualifies as "a company" except the three fund forms it excludes. An ETC/ETN issuer — invariably a special-purpose-vehicle public limited company (e.g. the WisdomTree, Boost, GraniteShares and Leverage Shares issuer entities are all "…plc" or "…Limited" entities with share capital, none of them an OEIC or a UCITS/NURS fund) — sits squarely inside the class reg 2 admits, and outside the class it excludes.

*"Recognised stock exchange"* (reg 2) *"has the same meaning as in section 1005 of ITA 2007"*, and HMRC's guidance restates the operative definition: *"'Recognised stock exchange' means: any market of a recognised investment exchange which is for the time being designated as a recognised stock exchange for the purposes of Section 1005 of the Income Tax Act 2007…"*, with *"listed on a recognised stock exchange"* meaning *"admitted to trading on that exchange and included in the official UK list maintained by the [FCA] as the UK Listing Authority."* The London Stock Exchange Main Market is the exchange HMRC's own guidance uses as its worked example elsewhere in the same document (the Depositary Interests section discusses *"international shares officially listed on the London Stock Exchange (LSE) or admitted to trading on… AIM"*), so there is no live question about whether the LSE itself is "recognised" — it plainly is.

## Does the qualifying-investments list reach a debt-security-structured ETN?

**Yes, via reg 7(2)(b) — "qualifying securities."** HMRC's guidance defines the underlying term directly:

> *"'Security' means any loan stock or similar security of a company whether secured or unsecured. Qualifying securities may therefore include: loans, loan stocks (whether secured or not), debentures, Eurobonds."*

and sets the qualifying test:

> *"Defined securities, other than securities in an investment trust, are qualifying investments if: they have been issued by a company that is incorporated anywhere in the world; they satisfy at least one of the following conditions: the securities are listed on the official list of a recognised stock exchange / the shares in the company issuing the securities are so listed / the company issuing the securities is a 75% subsidiary of a company whose shares are so listed… the securities are admitted to trading on a recognised stock exchange in the UK or the EEA / the shares in the company issuing the securities are so admitted to trading / the company issuing the securities is a 75% subsidiary of a company whose shares are so admitted to trading."*

An ETN is, structurally, exactly this: an unsecured or secured debt obligation of an issuer company, whose return is linked to an index or reference asset by contract rather than by fund ownership. Nothing in the reg 7(2)(b)/(5) test asks what the debt security's return is linked to. **The industry's own ISA-classification practice reads it the same way.** TISA's published note on a Leverage Shares 5x leveraged ETN states this explicitly, in language that tracks the regulation almost clause-for-clause:

> *"Exchange traded commodities (ETCs) and exchange traded notes (ETNs) are treated as securities for ISA qualification purposes and fall under the category of qualifying securities, i.e., to qualify for ISA inclusion, either the security or the shares in the issuing company need to be listed on a recognised stock exchange or admitted to trading on a recognised stock exchange in the [EEA] or the UK… This ETN/ETP i.e., the security is traded on the Börse Frankfurt (a recognised stock exchange) so on that basis, the security should qualify for stocks & shares ISA inclusion."*
> — TISA, `https://www.tisa.uk.com/investment/leverage-shares-5x-long-us-tech-100-etp-securities-xs2399364152/`

That note is about a **5x leveraged** ETN and reaches "qualifying" without leverage ever entering the analysis — TISA applies the same listing test HMRC's own guidance sets out, to a leveraged instrument, and gets the same answer as for an unleveraged one. This is a UK industry ISA-classification body's determination, not a broker's marketing claim, and not itself primary law — but it is independent corroboration that market practice reads reg 7(2)(b) exactly the way this doc derives it from the text. **Note the instrument in that TISA note is listed on Börse Frankfurt, not the LSE** — it corroborates the *test* reg 7(2)(b)/(5) applies to a leveraged ETN, not a TISA finding about the LSE specifically. The LSE-listing half of the analysis is carried independently, below, by the LSE's own admission factsheet.

## Does it reach a commodity-backed ETC?

**Yes, on the identical hook, with no separate provision for commodities at all.** ETCs are, in the same structural sense as ETNs, secured debt securities of an issuer company — collateralised by physical metal, or by a swap/derivative referencing a commodity or commodity index, rather than by a general unsecured claim. Neither SI 1998/1870 nor HMRC's guidance contains any regulation, sub-paragraph, or guidance passage that distinguishes an "ETC" from any other qualifying security by what collateralises or references it. The word "commodity" does not appear anywhere in reg 7 or in the HMRC guidance page fetched for this doc. TISA's own note treats "ETCs" and "ETNs" as a single category for this purpose — *"Exchange traded commodities (ETCs) and exchange traded notes (ETNs) are treated as securities for ISA qualification purposes and fall under the category of qualifying securities"* — because the regulation itself draws no line between them. **The absence of any ETC-specific rule is itself the answer**: reg 7(2)(b) does not ask what a security's collateral or reference asset is, so an oil ETC and a corporate Eurobond clear the same gate on the same terms.

## Does embedded leverage or an embedded derivative change the answer?

**No — and the regulation has no textual mechanism by which it could.** Reg 7(2)(b) and reg 7(5) test two things only: (1) is the issuer "a company" as reg 2 defines it, and (2) is the security (or a chain running through it) listed on, or admitted to trading on, a recognised stock exchange. There is no third condition anywhere in reg 7 — no cap on volatility, no restriction on the security's payout formula, no distinction between a security that pays a fixed coupon and one whose return is 3x a daily index move via an embedded total-return swap. The wrapper's *legal form* is what regulation 7 tests; the wrapper's *economic content* is invisible to it.

The strongest evidence for this is the one place the Regulations *did* decide to look at economic/asset-class content: the 2026 cryptoasset ETN carve-out. Regulation 7(2G) states *"A UK cryptoasset exchange traded note is not a qualifying security for the purposes of paragraph (2)(b)"* — a bespoke exclusion, drafted and inserted specifically, naming a specific underlying-asset category. HMRC's own policy paper on this change records the sequence that makes the general rule visible:

> *"Initially, cETNs will be automatically eligible for inclusion in stocks and shares ISAs"* [after the FCA lifted its retail-distribution restriction on UK-listed crypto ETNs, effective 8 October 2025] *"…From 6 April 2026, they will be reclassified as qualifying investments within the Innovative Finance ISA (IFISA)."*
> — `https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-exchange-traded-notes/tax-treatment-of-cryptoasset-exchange-traded-notes-policy`

HMRC's own ISA-manager bulletin, Tax-free savings newsletter 18 (November 2025), restates the identical sequence in near-identical words — *"From 8 October 2025 cryptoasset Exchange Traded Notes are automatically eligible as qualifying investments for... stocks and shares Individual Savings Accounts"*, and *"With effect from 6 April 2026, cryptoasset Exchange Traded Notes will be reclassified as qualifying investments for Innovative Finance ISAs only"* — so this is not a one-off phrasing in a single policy paper but HMRC's consistent, repeated characterisation of its own rule across two separate publications.

"Automatically eligible" is the load-bearing phrase: no ISA-regulation amendment was needed to *admit* crypto ETNs once the FCA's product-intervention ban on retail distribution lifted — they fell straight into the pre-existing reg 7(2)(b) "qualifying securities" test the moment a retail investor could lawfully be sold one, because that test was already sitting there, unlocked, waiting for any debt-security-structured, exchange-listed note. **That is only true if reg 7(2)(b) already reaches ETN-structured products generally.** Parliament and HMRC then had to go back and write a *specific*, dated, named exclusion (2G/2H) to pull crypto ETNs back out into the IFISA instead — precisely because the general rule would otherwise have kept including them. No equivalent exclusion has been written, or proposed, for leveraged equity-index or commodity ETNs/ETCs. Leverage and derivative-embedding are not a distinguishing fact pattern anywhere near this carve-out; the carve-out is about the reference asset being a cryptoasset, a policy concern (retail crypto-exposure-via-tax-wrapper) that has nothing to do with how much daily leverage a note carries.

## Contrast with UCITS ETFs — what actually differs

A UCITS ETF qualifies under **reg 7(2)(ga)**, not reg 7(2)(b): *"shares of a UK UCITS, a recognised UCITS or a non-UCITS retail scheme that are listed on or admitted to trading on a recognised stock exchange."* This is not a "better" or "more clearly eligible" category than qualifying securities — it is a **different eligibility route with a different upstream gate**. To use it at all, the issuing vehicle must first be a UK UCITS or recognised UCITS: per HMRC's guidance, *"a collective investment scheme authorised under section 31(1)(a) of [FSMA 2000] that complies with the requirements to be a 'UCITS scheme' for the purposes of the Collective Investment Schemes Sourcebook."* The London Stock Exchange's own admission factsheet confirms this is a structurally distinct pathway at the point of listing: ETFs *"must be either an FCA-authorised or FCA-recognised collective investment scheme and must take the legal form of an [AUT], [ICVC], or [ACS]"*, whereas — in the very same document, as a separate route — *"ETCs & ETNs can be admitted to trading on the London Stock Exchange via the route below: Apply to the FCA for a London listing and to London Stock Exchange for admission to trading on the Main Market."* No fund-authorisation step, no UCITS-compliance certificate, no ACS/ICVC/AUT legal form requirement — an ETC/ETN issuer is admitted as a *company* issuing *debt*, exactly matching reg 7(2)(b)'s test rather than reg 7(2)(ga)'s.

That upstream difference is also *why* ADR-0016's universe is ETC/ETN-structured rather than UCITS-wrapped in the first place: the UCITS Directive imposes global-exposure and leverage limits on a UCITS fund (diversification rules, counterparty exposure caps, and — via the commitment or VaR approach — practical ceilings on how much leveraged, single-underlying exposure a UCITS vehicle can carry) that a 3x single-index or single-commodity daily-leveraged product cannot cleanly satisfy at scale, which is precisely the physics doc 18 recorded as ruling out a broad UCITS tracker for an intraday take-profit. **So the regulation's difference in treatment is exactly wrapper structure, not a differing view on eligibility**: reg 7 offers two doors — a fund door, gated by fund regulation and leverage limits that exclude ADR-0016's instruments by construction, and a securities door, gated only by "is it a company" and "is it listed" — and ADR-0016's instruments walk through the second door because the first is structurally closed to them, not because the second door admits something lesser.

## The listing condition — is it satisfied, or itself in doubt?

**Satisfied structurally, and not the locus of doubt.** Regulation 7(5)'s listing conditions — (b) *"the securities are so listed"* [officially listed on a recognised stock exchange] and (e) *"the securities are so admitted to trading"* [on a recognised stock exchange in the UK or EEA] — describe exactly the process the London Stock Exchange documents for getting an ETC or ETN onto its own Main Market: *"Prior to any potential listing, please liaise with the [FCA]… in order to have the products' prospectus approved and stamped… ETCs & ETNs can be admitted to trading on the London Stock Exchange via the route below: Apply to the FCA for a London listing and to London Stock Exchange for admission to trading on the Main Market."* A line that is trading on the LSE Main Market under a TIDM has, definitionally, been through the FCA-listing-plus-Main-Market-admission process reg 7(5)(b)/(e) is testing for — an LSE Main Market ETC/ETN line typically satisfies both letters simultaneously (FCA listing *and* Main Market admission to trading), so there is no scenario in which it clears one and fails the other. There is no separate, harder listing bar specific to leveraged or ETC/ETN products that a plain-vanilla LSE line clears and a leveraged LSE line does not — the admission factsheet documents one route for "ETCs & ETNs" as a class, leveraged or not, and a different route (via FCA fund authorisation) for ETFs.

**What could, in principle, still fail this test on a specific line — narrow and checkable, not systemic:**
- A line trading only on a market segment HMRC's own guidance treats as "admitted to trading" rather than "officially listed" (the guidance gives AIM as its own example of this distinction, and TISA's note gives Börse Frankfurt the same treatment: *"don't meet the definition of 'listed'… but… do meet the definition of being 'admitted to trading'… so they are ISA qualifying for that reason"*). Both routes qualify under reg 7(5), so this affects nothing here — it is only relevant if a line traded on neither an official list nor an admitted-to-trading venue, which an LSE Main Market line by definition does not.
- Whether the specific issuing SPV is, for a specific line, actually a "company" as reg 2 defines it (share capital, not an OEIC/UCITS/NURS) — checked directly against one issuer's own base prospectus rather than left as inference. WisdomTree's current base prospectus (fetched and read in full for this doc; see Method and provenance) states this in terms: *"The Issuer is a public limited company incorporated in Jersey for the purpose of issuing WT Securities…"*, and separately: *"WT Securities are secured, undated, limited recourse debt securities issued by WisdomTree Issuer X Limited, a Jersey company established as a special purpose vehicle…"* — a public limited company (share capital by definition) issuing debt securities, exactly the reg 2/7(2)(b) fact pattern, confirmed from the issuer's own primary document rather than assumed from the entity's name. The other major LSE leveraged-ETP issuers (Boost/WisdomTree Boost, Leverage Shares, GraniteShares) were not individually checked against their own prospectuses in this pass, but all trade under "…plc" or "…Limited" issuer names on the same admission route, so nothing in this research suggests a different fact pattern for them.

## Scope — what this doc does and does not settle

This is a **regulation-level, venue-independent** finding: the law reaches ADR-0016's instrument class. It does not, and cannot, settle:

1. **Whether any specific broker's platform correctly codes a specific ADR-0016 ISIN as ISA-eligible.** Doc 35 found IBKR's own ISA permission profile reading *"Stock Only — No other asset types permitted"* and Saxo's ISA page naming *"stocks, bonds, ETFs and funds"* without naming ETCs/ETNs. Those are the venue's own instrument-master-data and marketing-copy choices, which this doc's finding suggests are **under-inclusive relative to what the law actually admits** — but a venue's internal system not recognising a legal category is an operational gap at that venue, not evidence the category is legally excluded.
2. **The FCA's separate retail-distribution appropriateness regime for complex products.** T212's "complex products questionnaire" (named in #665, covering ETCs, other ETPs, and leveraged/inverse funds) is a COBS/PRIIPs conduct-of-business check that a firm must run before it may sell a complex instrument to a retail client at all — a different statute, a different regulator function, and a different question from "is this a qualifying investment for the tax wrapper." A line can be ISA-qualifying under SI 1998/1870 and still be gated by a firm's appropriateness assessment before a retail investor is allowed to buy it, in or out of an ISA. Passing that questionnaire is a precondition to trading the line at all, independent of this doc's finding.
3. **#665 itself, which remains open and HITL-gated** — its own most recent comment records that it is *"Sequencing: do #896 before this one"* and unaffected by anything short of David completing the T212 checklist in person. This doc removes the *regulatory* uncertainty #665's body implicitly carried (whether the ISA rules reach these products at all) but does not perform, or shortcut, the HITL steps #665 still requires.

## Confidence and residual ambiguity

**High confidence on the core legal answer.** The "qualifying securities" hook (reg 7(2)(b)/(5)), its "company" definition (reg 2), and the absence of any leverage/derivative-content test anywhere in reg 7 were all read directly from the current in-force text of SI 1998/1870 on legislation.gov.uk, cross-checked against HMRC's own guidance restating the same test in near-identical language, and independently corroborated by an industry ISA-classification body (TISA) applying that exact test to a named leveraged ETN and reaching the same conclusion. The crypto-ETN carve-out — added for 6 April 2026 and thus very recent, deliberately narrow, and explained in HMRC's own policy paper as building on a pre-existing "automatically eligible" default — is unusually strong confirmatory evidence precisely because it shows the regulator's own drafting behaviour when it *does* want to exclude an ETN sub-category: a specific, named, dated carve-out, which does not exist for leveraged equity-index or commodity ETNs/ETCs.

**Residual ambiguity, all narrow and operational rather than legal:**

- No primary source in this pass names a specific ADR-0016 ticker (e.g. 3USL, or a named oil ETC) and states "this ISIN is ISA-qualifying" — the finding is that the *category* clears the test, verified structurally, against one issuer's own base prospectus (WisdomTree Issuer X Limited — confirmed "public limited company" issuing "secured, undated, limited recourse debt securities", directly on point for reg 2/7(2)(b)), and by an industry classification of a comparable (also-leveraged) instrument, not by a line-by-line HMRC or issuer confirmation of every ADR-0016 ticker or every issuer (Boost, Leverage Shares, GraniteShares prospectuses were not individually pulled). A five-minute check of each remaining candidate ISIN's own Final Terms and issuer prospectus would close this completely; nothing found here suggests any ADR-0016 candidate would fail it.
- **Resolved, not ambiguous:** this doc checked the `gov.uk/government/collections/isa-managers-guidance` collection page directly. It lists 27 ISA-manager guidance documents by name and contains no document titled "Guidance Notes for ISA Managers" or any GN-numbered manual; "Stocks and shares ISA investments for ISA managers" is the only stocks-and-shares-specific entry, confirming it is the current, sole, consolidated guidance rather than one of several documents this research might have missed.
- Whether the FCA's crypto-ETN retail-distribution unlock (October 2025) has any analogue still pending for some other product sub-type that could, by the same mechanism, later change eligibility for an ADR-0016 instrument, was not investigated — out of scope, and no evidence surfaced suggesting one is pending for leveraged equity/commodity ETPs. WisdomTree's own prospectus corroborates that the crypto case was doubly gated in a way leveraged equity/commodity ETPs never were: the FCA's January 2020 MiFID rule banned retail sales of *crypto-tracking* exchange-traded securities specifically (lifted only for Bitcoin/Ethereum-backed lines from 8 October 2025, per the same prospectus's own account) — a retail-distribution restriction that never applied to non-crypto ETC/ETN products, which have been retail-distributable throughout (subject to ordinary PRIIPs KID and appropriateness rules, not a blanket ban).
