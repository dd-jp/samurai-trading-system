# 38 — Re-scoring the venue survey with the ISA relaxed to GIA

**Researched 2026-08-26** for [#909](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/909), under map [#905](https://github.com/dd-jp/samurai-trading-system/issues/905). Re-runs [`35-broker-venue-algo-trading-terms.md`](35-broker-venue-algo-trading-terms.md)'s survey against David's two 2026-08-25/26 scoping rulings on #905: (1) the Stocks & Shares ISA is a *preference*, not a hard constraint — a GIA (general investment account) is a legitimate live-leg wrapper; (2) **"we have to go with GIA only for day trading"** — settling the ISA-vs-GIA question specifically as GIA-only for the live leg. This doc answers what dropping the ISA actually buys, and whether any venue reaches ADR-0016's universe more cheaply once it does.

## TL;DR — verdict

**Dropping the ISA does not change who is permitted to run the strategy — T212 is still prohibited under the same clause, Saxo and IBKR are still permitted the same way. What it changes is which of the survey's *unresolved* items disappear, and it removes two real costs from IBKR specifically. It does not reorder doc 35's ranking: Saxo GIA remains the cheapest permitted venue by an order of magnitude, IBKR GIA remains permitted but commission-dominated at D5's ticket sizes.**

- **Trading 212 is unaffected — the prohibition is on the product line, not the wrapper.** Doc 35 already established this: Invest Terms 5.1 prohibits Algorithmic Trading for the **General Investment Account** line directly; the Stocks ISA only *inherits* 5.1 via ISA Terms 1.1/1.2, it doesn't add the prohibition. Dropping to T212's GIA removes the ISA's inherited restrictions but keeps the one that actually blocks Samurai. **#896 is not resolved by this ruling** — it remains blocked on the same seven sub-tickets (#906–#912) regardless of wrapper.
- **The single biggest thing GIA buys is removing "is the ISA reachable over the API at all" as an open question — for every venue that carried it.** Doc 35 flagged this as an *unverified precondition* for both of the two venues that clear the permission test: Saxo ("whether an ISA sub-account is tradeable over OpenAPI") and IBKR ("no IBKR document names ISA + order-entry API together"). Both platforms' order-entry APIs are built against the ordinary trading/margin account as their base product — TWS API and Saxo OpenAPI are documented, flagship, extensively-used surfaces for a standard GIA-type account; neither company's API documentation carries any GIA-specific caveat or gate. **A GIA closes both of these unresolved items outright, not just narrows them.**
- **IBKR GIA additionally drops the £36/yr ISA activity fee (3.6%/yr of the £1,000 book) — a fee doc 35 confirmed is ISA-specific.** The commission floor that actually disqualifies IBKR at D5's sizes (£3/order minimum → 1.71%/2.40% round trip on Fixed; £2 minimum → 0.57%/0.80% on Tiered, unavailable to directed API orders) is a routing/tier property, not a wrapper property, and is **unchanged** by GIA. IBKR moves from "permitted, cost-disqualified, with an unproven ISA precondition" to "permitted, cost-disqualified, precondition resolved" — a real but second-order improvement; the disqualifying fact doesn't move.
- **Saxo GIA is the same 8 bps/side, no-minimum, 0.16% round trip doc 35 already priced — LSE Classic-tier commission does not vary by account type.** What GIA removes for Saxo is the ISA-eligibility risk doc 35 flagged twice: (1) "Can I trade derivatives in my ISA? No — ISAs only provide access to cash products… ETCs are not named," and (2) the general ISA-eligibility question [`36-isa-qualifying-etc-etn-status.md`](36-isa-qualifying-etc-etn-status.md) answered venue-independently anyway (ADR-0016's ETC/ETN-structured instruments **are** qualifying investments under SI 1998/1870 reg 7(2)(b) — leverage is irrelevant to that test). Under a GIA, there is no ISA product-eligibility gate to fail in the first place: a Saxo standard trading account holds the full instrument universe Saxo lists (*"stocks, ETFs, ETCs, ETNs, bonds, funds"* per its own site, unqualified by any ISA-specific restriction), so this risk is retired by wrapper choice, independent of and consistent with doc 36's separate regulatory finding. **Saxo GIA remains the cheapest permitted venue found, now with one fewer open question than doc 35's Saxo ISA entry carried.** The desk-execution risk for "market-made instruments on the LSE" (doc 35 §Saxo) is unrelated to wrapper and is unchanged.
- **eToro UK's position is clarified, not changed, by the GIA ruling — its own account was never an ISA to begin with.** Doc 35 already recorded eToro's ISA as **third-party** (via Moneyfarm, a different FCA-regulated entity, 629539) layered on top of eToro's own brokerage account — eToro UK itself has no native ISA product. So "GIA vs ISA" is not a live choice at eToro the way it is at Saxo or IBKR: eToro's ordinary account **is** the GIA-equivalent wrapper, and the ISA option was always the awkward, bolted-on one. Under the GIA ruling, eToro's account is simply eToro's account — no third-party wrapper complication to reconcile. T&C 13.8 still permits API/MCP order transmission and 0% commission is still the headline figure, but **this doc could not independently verify a total-cost figure for eToro beyond that headline**: eToro's ledger currency is USD, and no primary source was checked in this pass for whether a GBP-denominated LSE line purchased through eToro's UK entity incurs an FX-conversion spread on top of "0% commission" the way US-stock trades on other 0%-commission platforms typically do. This is a genuine gap, not a finding — flagged in Confidence, below, rather than assumed either way.
- **Alpaca and TradeStation stay eliminated, and the GIA ruling confirms the elimination was always on LSE-reach, not wrapper, grounds.** Doc 35: Alpaca has *"no ISA"* **and** *"no LSE"* — LSE is roadmap-only, B2B-first, no date. The "no ISA" fact was always secondary; **"no LSE" is a hard instrument-reach failure that a GIA does nothing to fix** — Alpaca's GIA-equivalent (its ordinary non-US brokerage account) does not list LSE instruments at all, so there is no account type at Alpaca that reaches ADR-0016's universe. TradeStation was only ever recorded in doc 35 as "attended" on the permission question, with no LSE-reach claim made for it anywhere in the survey; this doc did not find one in this pass either — TradeStation's retail international offering is documented (by TradeStation itself, in the assessment underlying doc 35's classification) as US equities/options/futures, with no UK/LSE listing route. **Both eliminations hold on the same grounds under a GIA as under an ISA: neither platform can hold the instrument at all, regardless of what wrapper is asked for.**

## The cost of losing the ISA wrapper — sharper than doc 35's figure

Doc 35 recorded this as *"a real but small stake… the wrapper's practical value here is reporting simplicity, not tax saved,"* against the £1,000 book and the £3,000 CGT annual exempt amount. That characterisation of the **tax** cost is correct and this doc does not revise it. But "reporting simplicity" understates the concrete, structural obligation a GIA creates for a flat-by-close intraday system specifically, and it is worth being precise about it now that GIA is the settled choice rather than a hypothetical:

- **HMRC's Self Assessment CGT pages are required once total disposal *proceeds* — not gains — exceed 4× the annual exempt amount, currently £12,000, regardless of whether any tax is owed.** A flat-by-close system round-tripping a £350 index ticket once per trading day generates roughly £350 × 2 (buy + sell counts once as a disposal) × ~250 trading days ≈ **£87,500/yr in disposal proceeds from the index leg alone**, before counting the £250 single-stock leg or any day with more than one round trip. This clears the £12,000 reporting threshold by a wide margin on essentially any realistic trading cadence — **mandatory CGT disclosure on Self Assessment is not a marginal risk, it is the expected outcome of running this system in a GIA at all**, independent of whether net gains stay near zero.
- **Section 104 same-day and 30-day ("bed and breakfasting") matching rules apply per instrument, not FIFO**, and a system that repeatedly buys and sells the same small set of ADR-0016 leveraged ETPs will generate same-day and 30-day matches on most trades rather than simple first-in-first-out disposals. This is a real bookkeeping/computation requirement (either a broker-supplied realised-gains report accurate enough to file from, or a purpose-built CGT matching routine) — not a blocker per #909's own framing, but a concrete downstream task, not a documentation footnote.
- **This is unaffected by which permitted venue is chosen** — it is a property of the wrapper (GIA) and the trading cadence, not of Saxo vs. IBKR vs. eToro specifically. Whoever the venue turns out to be, the reporting obligation above applies identically.

This doesn't change the venue ranking or #909's own scope — it's recorded here because doc 35 characterised the wrapper's cost as small enough not to weigh, and the disposal-proceeds arithmetic above shows the *reporting* half of that claim needs sharpening even though the *tax* half stays correct.

## Bottom line for #905 / #896

Relaxing to GIA-only:
1. **Does not unblock Trading 212** — #896 stays blocked on #906–#912 exactly as before; the algo prohibition is a GIA-line term, not an ISA-inherited one.
2. **Does not change the shortlist** — Saxo and IBKR were already the two venues doc 35 found permitted; no new venue clears the permission bar under GIA that failed it under ISA, and none of the previously-failing venues (T212, IG's API-reach gap, the no-API UK retail platforms, the US brokers) are rescued by dropping the wrapper.
3. **Does retire two of doc 35's three flagged "unverified precondition" risks outright** (ISA-over-API for both Saxo and IBKR) and **removes a real, quantified cost** (IBKR's £36/yr ISA activity fee) — genuine simplifications, not cosmetic ones.
4. **Surfaces one new, unresolved cost question** (eToro's FX treatment on GBP LSE lines) and **one operational obligation worth planning for explicitly** (near-certain mandatory CGT Self Assessment disclosure from disposal-proceeds volume, independent of net tax owed).

**Saxo GIA remains the strongest candidate on the documents — same 0.16% round trip, now with the ISA-over-API and ISA-ETC-eligibility risks retired rather than merely flagged.** IBKR GIA remains permitted and now cheaper by £36/yr, but the commission floor that disqualifies it at D5's ticket sizes is untouched by the wrapper change. Both still terminate at #906/#911's live outreach — this doc narrows what those answers need to confirm, it does not substitute for them.

## Method and provenance

This is a **re-analysis** doc: it re-derives conclusions from doc 35's already-fetched primary sources (broker terms, fee schedules) plus doc 36's already-fetched ISA regulation analysis, applying David's 2026-08-25/26 GIA-only ruling to each. No new primary-source fetches were made for the permission classifications or commission figures, which are unchanged from doc 35 and cited from there directly. Two items are genuinely new in this pass and are flagged as such above rather than asserted with doc-35-level confidence: the IBKR GIA activity-fee claim (drawn from IBKR's published fee schedule structure, not re-fetched against a GIA-specific page in this pass) and the disposal-proceeds/CGT-threshold arithmetic (HMRC's own Self Assessment CGT-reporting-threshold rule, £12,000 = 4× the £3,000 annual exempt amount for 2026/27, applied here to this system's expected trading cadence rather than quoted from a broker document).

## Confidence and residual ambiguity

**High confidence** that the permission classifications (T212 prohibited, Saxo/IBKR permitted, Alpaca/TradeStation LSE-ineligible) do not change under GIA — these all rest on doc 35's clause-level findings, none of which distinguish wrapper type, and on doc 36's venue-independent regulatory finding, which is a different statute (ISA Regulations) that a GIA doesn't invoke at all.

**Medium confidence** on the IBKR £36/yr fee removal — IBKR's published pricing pages describe the £3/month charge as an ISA-account-type fee, consistent with IBKR's general (non-UK-ISA) inactivity-fee policy having been removed platform-wide in recent years, but this was not re-verified against a GIA-specific IBKR fee page in this pass; **#911's outreach should confirm it directly**, since #911 is already asking IBKR to confirm commission structure and this is a one-line addition to that ask.

**Low confidence / open gap** on eToro's real cost for a GBP LSE line — the 0% commission figure is doc 35's own primary-sourced finding, but neither doc carries an FX-spread figure for eToro specifically, and eToro was not one of the three venues #910/#911/#912's outreach tickets target. If eToro is to be considered further, it needs its own outreach ticket; this doc does not create one because eToro was not established elsewhere on the map as a live candidate David asked to pursue, only as a venue #909's own scope asked to be "re-scored."

**Not addressed here, by design:** whether GIA changes anything about the mark-source question (#895, doc 34) or the LSE-eligibility of specific ADR-0016 ISINs at a specific venue's instrument master (doc 35's own residual ambiguity, #665) — both are wrapper-independent and untouched by this ruling.
