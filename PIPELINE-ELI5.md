# Samurai — how it decides to trade (explained like you're 5)

Six friends work together every time the system thinks about buying or selling a stock.

## 1. Analysts
A few "friends," each looking at the stock a different way — one watches news, one watches
the price chart, one watches patterns. Each writes down: "I think it'll go up" or "I think
it'll go down," and why.

## 2. Debate
The friends argue about it. "You're wrong because X." "No, you're missing Y." Like a group
chat fight, but useful — bad ideas get picked apart before real money is on the line.

## 3. Trader
After the argument, one friend makes an actual plan: buy or sell, how much money, at what
price.

## 4. Risk
The cautious friend checks the plan. "That's too much money on one bet" or "too dangerous,
no." Can shrink the trade or kill it outright.

## 5. Verdict
The final yes/no. Trade happens or it doesn't, and at what size.

## 6. Execution
The order actually gets sent to the real broker. Real money moves.

## Bonus: Feedback Loop (runs in the background)
After a trade finishes, the system checks who was right and who was wrong, and trusts the
right friends a bit more next time.
